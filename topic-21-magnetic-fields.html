Magnetic Fields & Electromagnetism

F = BIL sin θF = Bqv sin θB = F/(IL)Φ = BAflux linkage NΦV_H = BI/(ntq)
EXAMINER'S BRIEF

This chapter is where the vector rules start biting. The two weapon skills: F = BIL sin θ with its “force is only when the wire is crossing field lines” idea, and Fleming’s left-hand rule for direction — field, current and force are always mutually perpendicular. Nothing is scored on the motor effect, Hall voltage, or the forces between parallel wires!

1 · Concept of a Magnetic Field

A magnetic field exists wherever a magnetic pole experiences a force. Magnets exert forces on each other — the law of magnets: like poles repel, unlike poles attract.

TWO SOURCES

Make a magnetic field two ways: permanent magnet — electron orbits inside atoms and a ferrous material combine → whole magnet; or moving charges — a current (in MRI coils, superconductor, etc.).

All magnetic fields are created by moving charges. Even a bar magnet's field comes from the tiny current-loops of electrons circulating in its atoms. In non-magnetic materials those little fields cancel.

FIELD LINE RULES
  • Lines go out of north poles, into south poles (a convention).
  • Direction of a line = the direction a free north pole would move.
  • Field is strongest where lines are closest together.
  • Plot with iron filings or a plotting compass.
COMPASS SECRET

A compass lines up with the local field. Near a magnet the needle swings wildly; near the Earth-in-nowhere it points N. Field lines are continuous loops from N to S — always!

2 · Field Patterns Around Currents

An electric current's own field is easy to sketch — the three patterns below are standard exam marks.

STRAIGHT WIRE

Concentric circles centred on the wire. Getting further out, the circles spread apart ⇒ weaker field.

reversing the current reverses the field

SOLENOID / FLAT COIL

Looks like a bar magnet. Field lines are parallel + equally spaced inside (± uniform). At the ends they spread out (field ≈ half the centre value).

RIGHT-HAND RULE (straight wire)

Grip the wire, thumb = direction of conventional current; fingers curl = direction of the magnetic field.

RIGHT-HAND GRIP RULE (solenoid)

Grip the coil so fingers curl with the current; thumb points to the field inside the coil − the north pole. See the coil end-on: anti-clockwise current = north, clockwise = south.

ELECTROMAGNET

Put a soft iron (ferrous) core inside — it magnetises and multiplies the solenoid field by ~1000×. Perfect for electromagnets, scrapyard cranes, MRI — switch off the current and the field dies, unlike a permanent magnet.

3 · Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor

Place with a magnetic field; the fields interact and exert a force on the conductor. Called the motor effect — the basis of electric motors.

MAGNITUDE

F = BIL sin θ

θ = angle between field and conductor · use when θ = 90° → F = BIL

Only the component of B at right angles to the current counts — B sin θ. Parallel = no force; perpendicular = maximum (F = BIL).

FLEMING'S LEFT-HAND (MOTOR) RULE

First finger = Field · seCond finger = Current · thuMb = Motion

Hold thumb + first two fingers mutually at right angles. Point the First finger in the field direction and the seCond in the current direction: the thumb gives the direction of the force/motion.

WORKED

0.2 A wire, 2.5 m long, at 90° to 0.060 T → F = BIL = 0.06 × 0.2 × 2.5 = 3.0 × 10⁻² N.
Same wire tilted to 25°: F = 0.050 × 0.40 × 0.20 × sin 25° = 1.7×10⁻³ N (force perpendicular to the page).

4 · Magnetic Flux Density B

DEFINITION

Magnetic flux density B = force per unit current per unit length on a wire placed at right angles to the field.

B = F/(IL sin θ) → F = BIL sin θ

Bigger B = denser field lines. Unit: tesla (T) = N A⁻¹ m⁻¹ = kg A⁻¹ s⁻².

MEASURE B — CURRENT BALANCE

Wire of known L between magnets on a top-pan balance. Zero with no current; switch on → force F = mg from the scale change (Newton's 3rd: equal-and-opposite force presses the magnets). Then B = F/(IL).

MEASURE B — HALL PROBE

The Hall voltage is ∝ to flux density. Hold the probe flat with field lines at 90° to it → read B right off. Rotating 180° flips the reading by 2× the true field.

5 · Force on a Moving Charge

Current = moving charges, so a single charge in a field feels a force too.

DERIVATION

I = nq/t, conductor length L = vt ⇒

F = B I L = B × (nq/t) × (vt) = Bnqv — force on n particles. Divide by n:

F = Bqv sin θ

stationary charge: force = 0

DIRECTION

Fleming's left-hand rule. Remember: negative particles (electrons) travel opposite to conventional current — point the 2nd finger opposite the electron velocity!

CIRCULAR ORBIT

The force is always at 90° to velocity ⇒ provides centripetal force ⇒ circular path in a plane ⊥ to field:

Bqv = mv²/r  ⇒  r = mv/(Bq)

goes as v, B, q — and specific charge q/m = v/(Br)

WORKED

Speed 9.1×10⁶, B = 0.50 mT, radius 10.2 cm:
e/m = v/(Br) = 9.1e6/(0.5e-3×1.022) = 1.8×10¹¹ C kg⁻¹
m = 1.6e-19 / 1.8e11 = 9.0×10⁻³¹ kg ✓

NASA HELP

Bigger charge, bigger speed or stronger field → smaller radius. Heavier particle → larger radius. In a bubble chamber, curving tracks reveal charge (sign) and q/m (curvature) of the unknown particle.

6 · The Motor / Current in a Coil

Rectangular coil in uniform B: the two long sides feel equal opposite forces, an antiparallel pair (a couple).

TORQUE ON A COIL

F = BIL (per side) · τ = F × (perpendicular distance)

τ maximum in the plain of the coil, falls to zero when the coil is edge-on to the field. Add a commutator and current swaps each half-turn — the coil spins: a D.C. motor!

MOVING-COIL METER

Wire coil pivoted between the poles of a magnet, a spring and pointer. Current ↔ torque ↔ deflection — calibrated mirror scale for ammeters/voltmeters. The torque ∝ current makes the scale linear-ish.

CHECK IT

No force on the conductor parallel to the field. On a coil edge-on, both forces act along the plane — no turning effect of it.

7 · Forces Between Parallel Conductors

THE RESULT

Like currents attract · Unlike currents repel

force per unit length ∝ (I₁I₂)/separation

F/ L for two wires: B from #1 times current #2 = μ₀I₁I₂/(2π·d) — conceptually the field of one pushes the other (motor effect); Newton's 3rd pair.

WHY?

Think of field lines as elastic bands drawn taut. Two like currents → their circular fields cancel between the wires, so the outer field lines squeeze them together. Antiparallel → strong field between, and they push apart.

DEMONSTRATION

Two aluminium strips in series — same current in both = the strips bend together (pinch). Reversed by connecting the ends → strips move apart. Foil is flexible; wires would deform.

HISTORY NOTE

This force defines the amp − two 1 A conductors 1 m apart in vacuum push with 2×10⁻⁷ N m⁻¹. (Redefined in 2019 via elementary charge, but the idea stands.)

8 · The Hall Effect & Hall Probe

A thin slice of conductor with a current; magnetic field normal to the slice deflects the charge carriers sideways → charge piles up → a p.d. appears across the slice: the Hall voltage.

DERIVATION

Deflection grows until the magnetic force (Bqv) equals the electric force from the +/−piles (q·V_H/d):

qV_H/d = Bqv ⇒ V_H/d = Bv

With drift speed v, I = Anvq and A = t·d:

V_H = BI/(ntq)

n = number density of carriers · t = thickness of slab

WHY IT IS TINY

In metals n is huge → V_H very small. Convenient with semiconductors (small n) → detectable voltages. Also the sign of V_H tells you if carriers are + or −.

WORKED

Foil, t = 0.1 mm, I = 4.5 A, B = 85 mT, n = 6.0×10²⁸ m⁻³:
V_H = BI/(ntq) = 0.085 × 4.5 / (6.0e28 × 1.0e-4 × 1.6e-19) = 4.0×10⁻⁷ V.

HALL PROBE → MEASURE B

Keep I fixed: V_H ∝ B. Calibrate the meter in teslas. Rotate the probe for max reading = field normal to probe. The component normal is B·sinθ, so probe at 35° to a reading field gives V(measured) = V₀ sinθ.

9 · Magnetic Flux & Flux Linkage

FLUX

Magnetic flux = the product of flux density and area placed normal to the field lines.

Φ = BA (B ⊥ A) · at an angle θ: Φ = BA sin θ

Unit: weber (Wb) = T m². It's the “total number of field lines through the region”.

FLUX LINKAGE

If the flux threads a coil of N turns, each turn links the same Φ − so:

flux linkage = NΦ = BAN

this is the quantity whose rate of change drives Faraday's law (next topic)

WORKED

400 turns wound on a cylinder core, r = 3.0 cm, B = 1.4 T:
Φ = B·πr² = 1.4 × π(0.03)² = 4.0 mWb
linkage = NΦ = 1.6 Wb

SIN vs the PLANE

When the coil is flat the flux is max; when B lies in the plane of the coil, Φ = 0. Take θ as the angle between B and the normal to the loop if your formula uses cos!
