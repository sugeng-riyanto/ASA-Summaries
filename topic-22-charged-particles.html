Motion of Charged Particles

F = Bqv sin θr = mv/Bqp = Bqrv = E/Bvelocity selectorV_H = BI/(ntq)
EXAMINER'S BRIEF

Paper 4 loves two set-pieces here: the circular path of a particle in a uniform field (Bqv = mv²/r) and the velocity selector (balanced forces giving v = E/B). The key idea — the magnetic force does no work, it bends the path but never changes the speed, so no kinetic-energy line is ever needed. Master that and the whole chapter becomes geometry.

1 · The Bev Force

A charge q moving at speed v, at an angle θ to a uniform magnetic field, experiences a sideways force. It is the same force that pushes a wire (the motor effect) working on single particles.

MAGNITUDE

F = Bqv sin θ

at right angles: sin θ = 1 → F = Bqv

From F = BIL with I = q/t and L = vt we get F = B × (q/t) × (vt) = Bqv. For an electron q = e, so F = Bev — the “Bev force”.

IT DOES NO WORK

The force is always at 90° to the velocity, so it changes the direction of motion but never the speed. The kinetic energy of the particle is unchanged — no ½mv² story in this chapter.

2 · Direction — Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

THE RULE

First finger = Field  ·  seCond finger = Current  ·  thuMb = direction of Motion

  • Positive charge: second finger points the way the particle moves (conventional current).
  • Electron (negative): the conventional current runs opposite to the electron — point your second finger the other way.
WORKED

An electron moves right while a magnetic field points into the page. The electron's conventional current flows left. Left-hand rule: the force pushes the beam up the page, and the beam curves that way. Reverse B and it curves down — this is how oscilloscope and TV electron beams get steered.

3 · Circular Orbits — r = mv/(Bq)

Because the Bev force is always perpendicular to the velocity towards a centre, a particle entering at right angles to a uniform field travels in a circle.

THE KEY EQUATION

The magnetic force provides the centripetal force:

Bqv = mv²/r   ⟹   r = mv/(Bq)

Since momentum p = mv it is sometimes written p = Bqr (so r = p/Bq).

PROPORTIONALITIES
  • faster particles → bigger circles (r ∝ v)
  • heavier particles → bigger circles (r ∝ m)
  • bigger charge → smaller circles (r ∝ 1/q)
  • stronger field → smaller circles (r ∝ 1/B)
WORKED

Electron: v = 8.0×10⁶ m s⁻¹, B = 1.2 mT. r = (9.11×10⁻³¹ × 8.0×10⁶)/(1.2×10⁻³ × 1.60×10⁻¹⁹) = 3.8×10⁻² m ≈ 38 mm.

4 · Fine-Beam Tube & the e/m Ratio

THE SET-UP

An electron gun fires a beam; a uniform magnetic field bends it into a circle of radius r. Measure r (with a mirror scale against parallax), and also give the electrons a known energy.

SPECIFIC CHARGE

The p.d. Vca between cathode and anode accelerates each electron: eVca = ½mev².

e/me = v/(Br)

so measuring Vca, B and r fixes the ratio e/m = 1.76×10¹¹ C kg⁻¹

THEN

Millikan's oil-drop experiment found the value of e (charge is always an integer multiple of it). Combining Thomson's e/m with Millikan's e gave the electron mass: m = e ÷ (e/m) ≈ 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg.

5 · Reading the Tracks

Bubble-chamber tracks are the "photo album" of particle physics. A uniform B fills the chamber, so each moving charge carves the circle r = p/Bq.

READ THE PHOTO
  • Curve bends one way → charge positive; the other way → negative (with the field direction known).
  • No curve at all → uncharged.
  • Gentle curve = big momentum / heavy; tight spiral = light or slow. Spirals shrink as particles lose energy — a shrinking radius means energy is being lost.
REMEMBER

the magnetic force never speeds a particle up or slows it down. A shrinking spiral denotes energy lost by collisions or radiation, not by the magnetic field itself.

6 · Velocity Selector — v = E/B

Cross an electric field at right angles to a magnetic field and you get a speed filter — the heart of the mass spectrometer.

BALANCED FORCES

Electric force up: Fe = qE. Magnetic force down: Fb = Bqv. Undeflected when equal:

qE = Bqv   ⟹   v = E/B

Speed selected is independent of charge and mass — only the ratio E/B matters.

WHY IT FILTERS

Too-slow particles feel a weaker Bev → the electric force wins → they deflect one way. Too-fast particles feel a stronger Bev → magnetic wins → deflect the other way. Only the exact speed v = E/B passes straight through the slit.

WORKED

B = 0.30 T, E = 1.5×10³ V m⁻¹ → v = E/B = 1.5×10³/0.30 = 5.0×10³ m s⁻¹ passes straight through.

7 · Helical Motion & Mixed Fields

HELIX

If the velocity is at θ to B, split it: the component parallel to B (v cos θ) drifts along the field; the perpendicular part (v sin θ) circles. Combined → a helix.

WORKED

Tilting the electron beam at θ to B changes the circular path into a spiral/helix; how fast it spirals along depends on v cos θ.

8 · Hall Effect & Hall Probe

THE EFFECT

A slice of conductor carries current while a field is normal to it. Charge carriers get pushed sideways; charge piles against one face until its electric repulsion stops the drift sideways. A p.d. across the slice — the Hall voltage — is the "balance point" between the magnetic Bev force and electric force on each carrier: q·(VH/d) = Bqv.

FORMULA

VH = BI/(ntq)

using I = Anvq and A = t·d

  • VH ∝ B (that is why a probe can measure B)
  • metal → huge n → tiny VH; semiconductor → tiny n → big VH (probe uses the semiconductor)
  • sign of VH reveals whether carriers are + or −
WORKED

Aluminium foil, t = 0.10 mm, I = 4.5 A, B = 85 mT, n = 6.0×10²⁸ m⁻³: VH = 0.085×4.5/(6.0×10²⁸×1.0×10⁻⁴×1.6×10⁻¹⁹) = 4.0×10⁻⁷ V — tiny, as expected for a metal.
