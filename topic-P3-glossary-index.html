Glossary & Index

Define · State · ExplainDescribeShow that · SketchSuggestDetermine · Calculate
HOW TO USE

The command-word list tells you what the examiner expects in an answer. The glossary gives physics definitions in exam-friendly wording — quote these near-verbatim for "define" questions. The index points you to the topic page and the graph/experiment each appears in.

1 · Command Words

CommandWhat you must do
DefineGive the precise meaning — often the equation with symbols named (e.g. p = F/A, pressure = force per unit area).
StateGive a brief, concise sentence (a law/statement) with no explanation.
DescribeState the facts/procedure in words; mention the apparatus and key steps.
ExplainDescribe + give reasons: pair the physics with why (why, e.g., a current can flow).
CalculateUse the data to work out a value — show method, units, precision.
DetermineWork out a value possibly from a graph or apparatus (like calculate, but more "measured").
Show thatShow the steps that lead to a given result — the working is expected, not just the final number.
SketchRough graph/diagram — correct general shape, labelled axes, no need for exact values.
EstimateApproximation — order-of-magnitude maths acceptable; give your reasoning briefly.
SuggestFree invention, but consistent with the physics — a plausible explanation/design.
DeduceDraw a conclusion from the given evidence/data.
ComparePoint out similarities and differences with data to support.
WORD SCHEMATICS

“Explain” requires the reason, “describe” only the account. “State and explain” → one sentence states, next sentence explains. Don't use "because" lazily — say specifically what changes and why.

2 · Core Glossary (A–C)

TermDefinition (exam wording)
AccuracyHow close a measured value is to the true/correct value.
AmplitudeMaximum displacement (of a wave/oscillator from equilibrium).
Angular displacementAngle turned in radians — θ = s/r.
Angular velocityRate of change of angular displacement; ω = θ/t = 2π/T.
Atomic number (Z)Number of protons in a nucleus.
Binding energyMinimum energy needed to separate a nucleus completely into its constituent nucleons.
CapacitanceCharge stored per unit potential difference; C = Q/V.
CoherenceTwo waves maintain a constant phase relationship.
CoupleTwo equal, opposite, parallel forces producing rotation only; torque = F × separation.
CurrentRate of flow of charge; I = Q/t (A).

3 · Glossary (D–K)

TermDefinition
DensityMass per unit volume, ρ = m/V.
DisplacementDistance measured from a starting point in a specified direction (vector).
Doppler effectApparent change in detected frequency/wavelength when a source and observer move relative to each other.
EfficiencyUseful output power ÷ input power (×100%).
e.m.f.Energy converted per unit charge by a source of electrical energy; E = W/Q.
EnergyThe capacity to do work (J).
Field strength (g, E, B)Force per unit mass/charge/current−length at a point, gravitational→g, electric→E, magnetic→B.
Flux linkageProduct of magnetic flux and number of turns of the coil NΦ = N·BA cos θ.
FrequencyNumber of complete oscillations (or cycles) per second (Hz).
GradientChange in y ÷ change in x of a straight-line graph.
Half-lifeTime for the number of radioactive nuclei (or activity) to reduce to half its initial value.
Homogeneous equationEvery term has the same base units.
Hooke's lawForce ∝ extension of a spring (within elastic limit): F = kx.
ImpulseProduct of force and the time it acts; FΔt = change in momentum.
IntensityPower transmitted per unit area perpendicular to the wave.
Kelvin (K)SI unit of thermodynamic temperature; absolute zero = 0 K.

4 · Glossary (L–R)

TermDefinition
Latent heat (L)Energy needed to change the state of a substance without temperature change; Q = mL.
LuminosityTotal electromagnetic power radiated by a star (W).
Moment of a forceForce × perpendicular distance from the point (pivot) to the line of action.
MomentumMass × velocity (vector); p = mv.
Newton's III lawIf an object A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal & opposite force on A (same type, opposite directions, on different bodies).
Ohm's lawThe p.d. across a metallic conductor at constant temp is proportional to current: V ∝ I.
PeriodTime for one complete oscillation (T = 1/f).
PhasePosition of a point in the cycle at a moment, expressed as angle or fraction; constant phase ⇒ coherent.
Potential differenceEnergy transferred per unit charge: V = W/Q.
PowerRate of doing work / energy transfer: P = W/t = Fv.
PressureNormal force per unit area (Pa = N m⁻²).
Radiant flux intensityRadiant power per unit area normally through a surface (W m⁻²).
Random errorUncertainties that vary in both directions — reduced by repetition/averaging.
RefractionChange in wave direction on changing speed/medium.
Resultant forceThe single force that best represents all forces acting (vector sum).
ResistivityResistance of a 1 m cube of material measured across its opposite faces; ρ = RA/L.

5 · Glossary (S–Z)

TermDefinition
Specific heat capacityEnergy required to raise 1 kg of substance by 1 K; E = mcΔθ.
StressForce per unit area; σ = F/A. (Strain = ε = ΔL/L; Young modulus = σ/ε.)
Strong forceAttractive short-range force binding protons & neutrons in the nucleus.
Systematic errorPersistent error in one direction; repeats average will not remove it.
Terminal velocityConstant speed when resultant force on a falling object = 0 (drag + weight balance).
Time constant (RC)Time in which a capacitor's charge/p.d. falls to 37% of its initial value; τ = RC.
WeightForce of gravitational attraction on a mass: W = mg (N).
WorkEnergy transferred by a force: W = Fs cos θ.
Young modulusMeasure of stiffness: stress ÷ strain (Pa).
Zero errorInstrument measures non-zero when the true value is zero; subtraction corrects.

6 · Index of Experiments & Graphs

KEY EXPERIMENTS
  • Measurement of g — pendulum; free-fall. → T² vs L (gradient)
  • Young modulus of a wire — E from F vs ΔL gradient. → Matter
  • Resistivity ρ = RA/L — R vs L plot.
  • Internal resistance — V vs I graph (gradient = −r).
  • Capacitor discharge — ln V vs t slope −1/RC.
  • Double slit — λ = aΔx/D; grating — d sinθ = nλ.
  • Thermal — heating water with an electrical heater for c.
  • Radioactive decay — ln (count) vs t → λ.
GRAPHS YOU MUST RECOGNISE
  • F–x spring (line through origin, area = energy)
  • stress–strain (Young's curve; brittle/ductile labels)
  • I–V characteristics (ohmic, filament lamp, diode)
  • p–V isotherms & adiabatic curves
  • V vs I for a cell (E & −r from intercept/gradient)
  • a/m vs T binding energy per nucleon curve
  • Black-body (intensity–wavelength) curves
  • Hubble's v vs d (straight line, gradient H₀)
