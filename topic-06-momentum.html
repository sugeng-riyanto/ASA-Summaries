Momentum & Collisions

p = mvImpulse = FΔtF = Δp/ΔtΣp_i = Σp_fElastic vs Inelastic2D Vectors
EXAMINER'S BRIEF

Momentum is fundamentally a vector quantity. The most frequent loss of marks in structured papers arises from neglecting sign conventions in 1D rebounds and failing to resolve orthogonal components in 2D interactions. Furthermore, elite candidates must rigorously distinguish between the universal conservation of momentum and the conditional conservation of kinetic energy.

1 · Overview & Concept Map

Momentum bridges the gap between kinematics and dynamics. While Newton's laws describe the instantaneous effect of forces, the momentum-impulse theorem allows us to analyze complex interactions over time without needing to know the exact force profile at every millisecond.

2 · Linear Momentum & Impulse

LINEAR MOMENTUM

The linear momentum of an object is the product of its mass and its velocity.

p = mv

Unit: kg m s⁻¹  or  N s  ·  Vector quantity (direction matches velocity).

IMPULSE

Impulse is the product of the average resultant force and the time interval over which it acts. It equals the change in momentum.

J = FΔt = Δp = mv − mu

Unit: N s  ·  Vector quantity.

SOOCRATIC INQUIRY

Consider a car crash. The change in momentum (Δp) is fixed by the car's mass and initial speed. How do crumple zones and airbags reduce the severity of injury without altering the total impulse required to stop the vehicle? (Hint: Analyze the relationship between F, Δp, and Δt).

3 · Newton's Laws Revisited

Newton originally formulated his laws in terms of momentum, not the simplified F = ma often taught at lower levels. The momentum formulation is more fundamental because it accounts for systems where mass changes over time (e.g., rockets expelling fuel).

LawMomentum FormulationImplication
First Lawp = constant if ΣF = 0An object maintains its momentum unless acted upon by a resultant external force.
Second LawF = Δp / ΔtThe resultant force is directly proportional to the rate of change of momentum.
Third LawF_AB = −F_BAWhen two bodies interact, their changes in momentum are equal and opposite (Δp_A = −Δp_B).
CRITICAL DISTINCTION

The equation F = ma is a special case of Newton's Second Law that only applies when the mass m is constant. For a rocket burning fuel, F = Δp/Δt must be used, as both v and m are changing.

4 · The Principle of Conservation of Momentum

CONSERVATION PRINCIPLE

In an isolated system (one with no external resultant forces acting on it), the total linear momentum before an interaction is exactly equal to the total linear momentum after the interaction.

Σp_initial = Σp_final

SOOCRATIC INQUIRY

When a falling apple hits the ground and stops, its momentum becomes zero. Has the principle of conservation of momentum been violated? (Hint: Define your system boundaries rigorously. What else is interacting with the apple?)

The Vector Nature of Momentum

Because momentum is a vector, direction matters. In 1D problems, you must establish a strict sign convention (e.g., right is positive, left is negative) before substituting values into the conservation equation.

Equation: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ (Notice that u₂ is negative if it moves left).

5 · Elastic & Inelastic Collisions

While momentum is always conserved in an isolated system, kinetic energy is not. The behavior of kinetic energy dictates the type of collision.

Collision TypeMomentumKinetic EnergyCharacteristics
Perfectly ElasticConservedConservedNo energy lost to heat/sound. Relative speed of approach = relative speed of separation.
InelasticConservedNOT ConservedSome KE is transformed into internal energy, sound, or deformation.
Perfectly InelasticConservedMaximum LossObjects stick together and move with a common final velocity.
ELASTIC COLLISION SHORTCUT

For a perfectly elastic collision in 1D, you can bypass the quadratic kinetic energy equation by using the relative velocity rule:

u₁ − u₂ = v₂ − v₁

Relative speed of approach = Relative speed of separation

SOOCRATIC INQUIRY

Consider a perfectly inelastic collision between a moving mass m and a stationary target mass M. By combining the conservation of momentum and kinetic energy equations, deduce the fraction of kinetic energy lost. What mass ratio (m/M) maximizes this energy dissipation?

6 · Explosions & 2D Collisions

Explosions

An explosion is simply a collision in reverse. The system starts at rest (total momentum = 0) and fragments fly apart. Total momentum remains zero, but kinetic energy increases, sourced from chemical or elastic potential energy.

0 = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂   ⇒   m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂

2D Collisions (Vector Resolution)

Momentum is conserved independently in all orthogonal directions. For a 2D interaction, we must resolve vectors into x and y components.

2D CONSERVATION EQUATIONS

x-axis: Σp_x (initial) = Σp_x (final)

y-axis: Σp_y (initial) = Σp_y (final)

VECTOR TRIANGLES

In an isolated 2D system, the initial momentum vector and the final momentum vectors form a closed vector polygon. If three objects are involved, they form a closed triangle.

7 · Worked Examples & Exam Practice

EXAMPLE 1 · IMPULSE & AVERAGE FORCE

A tennis ball of mass 60 g is served at 55 m s⁻¹. The racket is in contact with the ball for 25 ms.

Δp = mv − mu = 0.060 × 55 − 0 = 3.3 kg m s⁻¹.

F = Δp / Δt = 3.3 / 0.025 = 132 N.

EXAMPLE 2 · RECOIL (EXPLOSION)

A 1500 kg cannon fires a 5.0 kg cannonball horizontally at 70 m s⁻¹.

Initial momentum = 0. Final momentum = 1500V + 5.0(70) = 0.

1500V = −350 ⇒ V = −0.23 m s⁻¹ (recoils backwards).

EXAMPLE 3 · ELASTIC COLLISION SHORTCUT

A 2.0 kg ball moving at 4.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 3.0 kg ball elastically.

Approach speed = 4.0 − 0 = 4.0 m s⁻¹. Separation speed = v₂ − v₁ = 4.0.

Momentum: 2(4) + 0 = 2v₁ + 3v₂ ⇒ 8 = 2v₁ + 3v₂.

Substitute v₂ = v₁ + 4: 8 = 2v₁ + 3(v₁ + 4) ⇒ 8 = 5v₁ + 12 ⇒ v₁ = −0.80 m s⁻¹.

v₂ = −0.80 + 4 = 3.2 m s⁻¹.

EXAMPLE 4 · 2D SNOKER COLLISION

A white ball (mass m, u = 0.50 m s⁻¹) strikes a stationary red ball (mass m). After impact, they move off at 90° to each other, symmetrically at 45° to the original path.

Resolve along the initial path (y-axis):

mu = mv cos 45° + mv cos 45° ⇒ u = 2v cos 45°.

0.50 = 2v (0.707) ⇒ v = 0.35 m s⁻¹ for both balls.

8 · Mistakes · Tips · Tricks

✗ Mistake✓ Correction
Ignoring signs in 1D rebound problems.If a ball hits a wall and bounces back, Δp = mv − (−mu) = m(v + u). The change in momentum is large!
Assuming KE is conserved in all collisions.KE is only conserved in perfectly elastic collisions. In macroscopic impacts, it is almost always inelastic.
Using F = ma for rockets or conveyor belts.Use F = Δp/Δt. For a rocket, F = v(dm/dt) where dm/dt is the rate of fuel ejection.
Confusing Impulse with Work.Impulse = FΔt (changes momentum). Work = Fs (changes energy). They have different units (N s vs J).
EXAM TECHNIQUE
  • Define your system: Always explicitly state "Considering the system of A and B, external forces are negligible..." to justify conservation.
  • Draw a diagram: Draw "Before" and "After" sketches with velocity vectors and a clearly marked positive direction axis.
  • Check limits: If your 1D collision results in the rear object moving faster than the front object, they have passed through each other (physically impossible). Re-evaluate your math.

9 · One-Page Revision

MOMENTUM & IMPULSE

p = mv (vector)
J = FΔt = Δp
Area under F-t graph = Impulse
Units: kg m s⁻¹ or N s

NEWTON'S LAWS

1st: p = constant if ΣF = 0
2nd: F = Δp/Δt (Fundamental)
F = ma (Constant mass only)
3rd: Δp_A = −Δp_B

CONSERVATION

Σp_initial = Σp_final
Applies to isolated systems
Must use vector components
Sign convention is mandatory

COLLISIONS

Elastic: KE conserved
u₁ − u₂ = v₂ − v₁
Inelastic: KE lost to heat/sound
Perfectly Inelastic: Objects stick

EXPLOSIONS

Initial p = 0, Final p = 0
Objects fly apart
KE increases (from chemical/elastic potential energy)

2D RESOLUTION

Σp_x (initial) = Σp_x (final)
Σp_y (initial) = Σp_y (final)
Use vector triangles or trigonometry

KEY EXAM COMMAND WORDS

Deduce (use algebra to prove a collision is elastic) · State (define momentum as a vector) · Explain (use Newton's 3rd law to justify why momentum is conserved during an impact).
