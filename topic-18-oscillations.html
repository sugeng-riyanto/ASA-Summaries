Oscillations

Amplitude · Periodx₀ sin ωt · v₀ cos ωta = −ω²xEnergy ↔DampingResonance
EXAMINER'S BRIEF

Practically everything in this chapter hangs off one definition — an object moves with S.H.M. if its acceleration is directly proportional to its displacement from equilibrium, and is always directed towards equilibrium (a = −ω²x). From that you get x = x₀ sin ωt, v = v₀ cos ωt, and the energy swap. The most common exam slips? Leaving the calculator in degrees instead of radians, and confusing «period» with «amplitude». Get those right and it’s easy marks.

1 · Describing Oscillations

Anything moving back and forth repetitively around an equilibrium position is oscillating.

QuantityMeaning
Displacement xdistance & direction from equilibrium (can be ±)
Amplitude x₀maximum displacement from equilibrium
Period Ttime for one complete oscillation (s)
Frequency fnumber of oscillations per unit time = 1/T (Hz)
Angular frequency ωchange of phase per second = 2πf = 2π/T (rad/s)

In any complete cycle the phase changes by 2π rad. So in f oscillations per second the phase changes by 2πf rad s⁻¹ = ω.

PERIOD ~ FREQUENCY

T = 1/f    ω = 2πf = 2π/T

EXAMPLE

From a displacement–time graph with T = 0.40 s, x₀ = 0.02 m:
f = 1/T = 2.5 Hz, v₀ = ωx₀ = (2π×2.5)(0.02) ≈ 0.31 m/s.

2 · Free & Forced Oscillations

FREE

Oscillator vibrates at its natural frequency after one disturbance (guitar string plucked, pendulum pushed once). Frequency governed by its own characteristics — spring stiffness, length.

Forced

Driven by a periodic external force at the driver's frequency, not the natural. e.g. your body vibrating with the bus engine.

guitar string = freebus vibration = forceddoor on hinges = free

3 · Simple Harmonic Motion

DEFINITION

An object moves with S.H.M. if its acceleration a is directly proportional to its displacement x from equilibrium, and is opposite in direction to x.

So the acceleration is towards the equilibrium — a “restoring” force.

a = −ω²x

+2  Pull a pendulum sideways → the component of gravity pulls it back.
▼
Release → accelerates toward the centre (v increases, a decreases).
▼
At the centre: v maximum, a = 0.
▼
Passes centre, slowing toward the far side → a now acts the other way.
▼
At max displacement x=+x₀: v = 0, a maximum (−ω²x₀).
NOT S.H.M.

A basketball bouncing — contact is impulsive, acceleration isn't a smooth a∝−x. On a trampoline your feet lose contact — no continuous restoring force. S.H.M. needs gradual, continuous changes.

4 · Kinematics — Graphs of S.H.M.

  • x–t: sine — x = 0 at t=0 if starting from centre.
  • v–t: cosine — v max at centre, zero at extremes.
  • a–t: negative sine — inverted, since a ∝ −x.
CONNECTIONS

velocity = gradient of x–t graph; acceleration = gradient of v–t. Max speed is where x = 0, max acceleration where |x| = x₀.

5 · Angular Frequency & Equations

ω = angular frequency = phase sweep per second in radians. One oscillation covers 2π rad.

QuantityFormula
Angular frequencyω = 2πf = 2π/T
Displacementx = x₀ sin ωt   (centre, moving +, at t=0)
Displacement (start at max)x = x₀ cos ωt
Velocityv = v₀ cos ωt (= dx/dt)
Max speedv₀ = ωx₀
Speed at any xv = ±ω√(x₀² − x²)
Accelerationa = −ω²x
PHASE DIFFERENCE

Two identical oscillators, time separation t = 17 ms, T = 60 ms:
phase fraction = 17/60 ≈ 0.28 of a cycle → Δφ = 0.28 × 2π ≈ 1.8 rad.

6 · Calculations with S.H.M.

WORKED 1

Pendulum f=1.5 Hz, x₀=0.10 m, passes centre at t=0 → x = 0.10 sin (3.0πt) with t=0.50 s.
x = 0.10 × sin(3.0π × 0.5) = 0.10 × sin(1.5π) = −0.10 m — leftmost position.

WORKED 2

Piston 4200 rev/min, x₀ = 12.5 cm = 0.125 m:
f = 4200/60 = 70 Hz   ω = 2π×70 ≈ 4.4×10² rad/s
v₀ = ωx₀ ≈ 55 m/s   max a = ω²x₀ ≈ 2.4×10⁵ m/s².
F = ma = 0.24 × 2.4×10⁵ ≈ 5.8×10⁴ N.

RADIAN MODE!

Always set the calculator to radians (RAD) before computing sin ωt. A degree-mode error gives nonsense — a classic examiner trick.

7 · Energy in S.H.M.

  • At the extremes (x = x₀): energy stored as potential (elastic/gravitational).
  • Through the centre: energy is all kinetic.
  • The energy swings between the two forms, total constant if undamped.
TWO peak per cycle

Both KE and PE go through two maxima in each period (once per side!). Max KE = ½mv₀² = ½mω²x₀².

EXAMPLE — BOB

0.40 kg pendulum bob, T=2.0 s, x₀=0.15 m, v₀ = (2π/2.0)(0.15) ≈ 0.47 m/s → max KE = ½ × 0.40 × (0.47)² ≈ 0.044 J & matching max PE = ½ mω²x₀² ≈ 0.044 J.

8 · Damping

WHAT IT DOES

Resistive force (friction, air resistance) removes energy → amplitude decays with time. Period remains unchanged.

TYPES
  • Light: many swings, small decay each cycle.
  • Heavy (over): returns very slowly, never passes equilibrium.
  • Critical: exactly enough to return to equilibrium without oscillating — cars suspension, door closers.
QUANTITATIVE

Amplitude falls via an exponential envelope. Define a “half–life” of amplitude — time to halve initial amplitude.

9 · Resonance

DEFINITION

Resonance = the large buildup of amplitude when a system is forced to oscillate at its natural frequency.

forcing frequency f_dri = f_natural ⟶ amplitude → maximum

USEFUL

child on a swing • guitar & radio tuning • microwaves aligning water molecules (2.45 GHz) • MRI & ultrasound.

PROBLEMS

Millennium Bridge (coupled to walking pace) • buildings shaking during earthquakes • machine parts flexing near resonance.

RESONANCE CURVE

Amplitude peaked at natural freq; damping lowers & widens the peak. Too much damping — amplitude never builds.
