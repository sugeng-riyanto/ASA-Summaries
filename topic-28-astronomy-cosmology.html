Astronomy & Cosmology

EXAMINER'S BRIEF

This topic puts Doppler effect, black-body radiation and the electromagnetic spectrum to cosmic scale. The three workhorse equations: F = L/(4πd²), λ_max T = constant and L = 4πσr²T⁴, then Δλ/λ ≈ v/c and v ≈ H₀d. Two killer skills: using the Sun as a reference star (no constants needed!) and converting km s⁻¹ per Mpc into s⁻¹.

1 · Luminosity & Radiant Flux

A star's luminosity L is the total power of electromagnetic radiation it emits — the total radiant energy per second, measured in W (e.g. the Sun: L⊙ ≈ 3.9×10²⁶ W).

Radiant flux intensity F (apparent brightness) is the radiant power passing normally through unit area — what we actually measure on Earth, in W m⁻².

BRIGHT vs LUMINOUS

A 5 W lamp looks dimmer than a 3 W lamp placed closer. A star can be bright because it's luminous, or merely close. We cannot tell distance from appearance alone — that's why we need standard candles.

2 · The Inverse Square Law

A star's power spreads uniformly over a notional sphere of radius d. The sphere's area is 4πd², so the radiant flux intensity is:

INVERSE SQUARE LAW

F = L / (4πd²)

double the distance → quarter the flux · triple → one-ninth

Assumptions: the star radiates uniformly, and there is negligible absorption along the path (dust — always slightly wrong, hence "estimate").

GPS OF THE COSMOS?

Measure F with a telescope, find L from other physics, and d = √(L/(4πF)). This is the whole trick of distance-finding: know luminosity, read brightness, then solve for distance.

3 · Standard Candles

DEFINITION

A standard candle is an astronomical object of known absolute luminosity. What we do is measure its apparent brightness F, then knowing L, compute d.

CEPHEID VARIABLES
  • Stars whose radius (and luminosity) pulsates with a well-defined period (1–100 days).
  • Period–luminosity relationship (Leavitt, 1908): brighter Cepheids have longer periods.
  • Measure the period on Earth → read off L → combine with F → distance.
  • Distances to galaxies containing a Cepheid follow (e.g. Andromeda ≈ 2.5 Mly).
DISTANCE LADDER

Cepheids work out to ~tens of Mly (Earth-bound). For truly vast galaxies, step up to Type 1a supernovae — implosions of white dwarfs whose peak luminosity is (nearly) constant, so they act as even brighter candles. Every rung above is calibrated against the one below.

4 · Wien’s Displacement Law

The hotter an object, the shorter the wavelength of peak black-body emission and the bluer it looks: "white-hot", then "red-hot" as it cools.

WIEN’S DISPLACEMENT LAW

λ_max T = constant = 2.9×10⁻³ m K

λ_max = peak wavelength · T = surface temperature

Black bodies (stars behave approximately so) absorb everything; their spectrum depends on T alone.

Sun: λ_max ≈ 500 nm → T = 2.9×10⁻³/500×10⁻⁹ ≈ 5800 K. Blue stars are hot; red ones cool.

5 · Stefan–Boltzmann Law & Stellar Radii

STEFAN–BOLTZMANN LAW

L = 4πσ r² T⁴

power radiated ∝ surface area × T⁴ ·  σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴

The T⁴ dependence is brutal: a star twice as hot radiates 2⁴ = 16× as much per square. That's why a cool red giant (huge r, modest T) can outshine the Sun while being cooler — a giant area beats a hot one.

MEASURING A STAR'S RADIUS — RECIPE
  1. Wien: measure λ_max of its spectrum → find T (using the constant 2.9×10⁻³ m K).
  2. Flux + distance: F = L/(4πd²) → find L.
  3. Stefan–Boltzmann: r = √(L/(4πσT⁴)).
REFERENCE-STAR SHORTCUT FOR EXAMS

Compare with the Sun: T_★/T_☉ = λ_☉/λ_★ (Wien) and L_★/L_☉ = (r_★/r_☉)² (T_★/T_☉)⁴ — constants cancel, fewer numbers to juggle.

6 · Redshift & the Doppler Equation

A distant galaxy's absorption spectrum shows the same dark lines as the lab's — but shifted. When observed wavelengths are longer than lab values → redshift → source is receding. Shorter → blueshift → approaching. (Only the velocity along the line-of-sight shows up.)

DOPPLER REDSHIFT

Δλ/λ₀ ≈ Δf/f ≈ v/c

Δλ = λ_observed − λ₀ (m) · only the line-of-sight component; ≈ for v ≪ c

SIGN CONVENTION

Δλ+ (longer) → receding → v positive; negative → approaching. Beware: use the same units top and bottom of Δλ/λ₀ — nm over nm cancels, so don't over-convert.

7 · Hubble’s Law

THE BIG PICTURE

Hubble (1929) plotted the radial velocity of nearby galaxies (from redshift) against their distances (from Cepheids): a straight line through the origin. The farther a galaxy, the faster it recedes → the Universe is expanding.

HUBBLE’S LAW

v ≈ H₀ d

gradient of the graph = H₀ · 75 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ ≈ 2.4×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹

UNIT CONVERSION

1 Mpc = 3.09×10¹⁹ km · so H₀ = 75 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ → (75×10³)/(3.09×10²²) ≈ 2.4×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹. Doing this in SI is a favourite exam 2-marker.

AGE OF THE UNIVERSE ≈ 1/H₀

If galaxies receded at v = H₀d for the whole history, the time for all of them to leave a point is T = d/v = 1/H₀. With H₀ = 2.4×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹ → ≈ 4×10¹⁷ s ≈ 1.3×10¹⁰ y (≈ 13–14 billion years). (With different H₀'s, 11–20 Gy.)

8 · The Big Bang & CMBR

THE MODEL

The Cosmological Principle: on large scales the Universe is homogeneous (uniform) and isotropic (looks the same in every direction) — no centre, no edge; every galaxy sees every other receding. Redshifted light shows the fabric of space itself is stretching: wavelength increases as space expands.

① Re-run the expansion backwards — all galaxies converge.
▼
② The Universe began ~13.8 billion years ago, tiny, hot and dense (the Big Bang).
▼
③ Space has expanded ever since — so distant galaxies recede faster, exactly as Hubble saw.
COSMIC MICROWAVE BACKGROUND (CMBR)

T ≈ 2.7 K   ↔   λ_max ≈ 1 mm (microwaves)

The afterglow of the Big Bang floods the sky from every direction. Use Wien's law with λ_max ≈ 1 mm → T ≈ 3 K — just as predicted. Its existence + black-body shape is the smoking-gun evidence for the Big Bang.

Age (Universe) ≈ 1/H₀ ≈ 13–14 Gy

DARK MATTER (qualitative)

Galaxies rotate too fast — the visible matter alone can't provide the extra gravity holding them together. Dark matter: matter that doesn't emit or absorb light but shows up gravitationally. It outweighs normal matter several-fold; candidate particles (WIMPs) and its true nature are still unknown.

9 · Worked Examples

SOLAR FLUX AT EARTH

L = 3.8×10²⁶ W, d = 1.5×10¹¹ m: F = L/(4πd²) = 3.8×10²⁶/(4π×(1.5×10¹¹)²) ≈ 1.4×10³ W m⁻².

CEPHEID DISTANCE

L = 4.6×10¹⁵ W, F = 1.3×10⁻²³ W m⁻²: d = √(L/(4πF)) = √(4.6×10¹⁵/(4π×1.3×10⁻²³)) ≈ 5.3×10¹⁸ m ≈ 560 ly.

SUN'S TEMPERATURE

λ_max = 510 nm → T = 2.9×10⁻³/510×10⁻⁹ ≈ 5700 K. (Table says 5800 K; exam-friendly.)

REDSHIFT SPEED

Lab line 486.133 nm, observed 486.112 nm → Δλ = 0.021 nm, negative (bluer): v = c·Δλ/λ = 3×10⁸×0.021/486.133 ≈ 1.3×10⁴ m s⁻¹ toward Earth.

TRAP LIST
  • ✗ confusing L and F — the Sun's L is fixed, F falls off d².
  • ✗ forgetting to convert km to m (or Mpc to m) before using H₀ in s⁻¹.
  • ✗ Δλ numerator vs λ₀ denominator — both must be the same units (keep nm).
  • ✗ blueshift sign — a negative Δλ means approaching, red is receding.
  • ✗ line-of-sight only — Doppler from spectra finds radial speed.
  • ✗ mixing luminosity (constant) with flux.
