EXAMINER’S BRIEF

Gravity is always attractive, acts from the centre of mass, and obeys an inverse square law. The whole topic pairs one force law F = GMm/r² with two per-unit-mass bookkeeping quantities: field strength g = force per unit mass (N kg⁻¹ = m s⁻²) and potential Φ = energy per unit mass (J kg⁻¹, taking zero at infinity). Orbiting is just the gravitational force playing the role of the centripetal force you met in topic 13.

1 · Gravitational Fields & Field Lines

DEFINITIONS

Gravitational field = region of space where a mass experiences a gravitational force.

Field strength g at a point = the force per unit mass on a small test mass placed there.

g = F/m   [N kg⁻¹ = m s⁻²]

N kg⁻¹ is the same as m s⁻² — g doubles as the acceleration of free fall.

  • Field lines show the direction of the force on a mass (the arrow) and the field strength (spacing).
  • Lines never touch or cross; they are smooth curves. Closer lines = stronger field.
  • Earth’s field is radial far away; on the scale of a building it is uniform (parallel, evenly spaced lines).
  • A sphere behaves as if all its mass were at its centre of mass (for points outside it). This tidies every calculation into point-mass form.
BUILDING SCALE

In and around a building the lines are effectively parallel and equally spaced — the field is uniform over a few metres, so your weight is the same at ground floor and top floor. That is why W = mg and mgΔh work so well near the surface.

2 · Newton’s Law of Gravitation

THE LAW

Two point masses attract each other with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.

F = G·m₁m₂ / r²

G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻² (universal) · r measured between centres of mass

  • Force is attractive, along the line joining the masses. The two pulls are equal and opposite (Newton’s 3rd law).
  • Inverse square: double the distance → quarter the force. The field lines spread over four times the area, so their concentration falls to ¼.
  • Valid for uniform spheres too, if r = centre-to-centre distance (> sum of radii).
IT’S WEAK

Between laboratory masses the force is absurdly small: two 100 g objects 1.0 cm apart feel only F = 6.67e-11 × 0.01 / 0.01² ≈ 6.7 × 10⁻⁹ N — that is why Cavendish’s torsion balance was needed to measure G. Yet gravity dominates the cosmos because it only attracts and never cancels.

FEEL THE NUMBERS

Sun–Earth: F = G(2.0e30)(6.0e24)/(1.5e11)² ≈ 3.6 × 10²² N. Moon on a 1.0 kg mass: 2.7 × 10⁻³ N kg⁻¹ — Earth pulls 175× harder, yet the Moon still drives the tides by its differential pull across the Earth.

3 · Field Strength: g = GM/r²

Field strength is force per unit mass — so divide the gravitational force between M and m by the mass m and most of the algebra vanishes, leaving only the field-producing mass M and the distance r:

DERIVATION TO LEARN

F = GMm/r²; g = F/m ⇒

g = GM / r²

vector, pointing towards the mass that makes the field. Inverse square in r.

doubling r → g ÷ 4halving r → g × 4independent of the mass inside the field
WHY g IS CONSTANT NEAR EARTH

At the surface, r = R ≈ 6.4 × 10⁶ m. Climb 10 km: r changes by 10 km/6400 km ≈ 0.16%, so g changes by ~2×(0.16%) ≈ 0.3%. Over a building or an airliner’s climb, g is essentially constant — mgΔh still works.

MEASURE EARTH’S MASS

g = GM/R² → M = gR²/G = 9.81 × (6.4e6)² / 6.67e-11 ≈ 6.0 × 10²⁴ kg. Then density ρ = 3M/4πR³ ≈ 5500 kg m⁻³ — that heavy hint that Earth has a dense metal core.

ABOUT DENSITY

Jupiter is 320 Earth masses and 11.2 Earth radii: gJ = 9.81 × 320/11.2² ≈ 25 N kg⁻¹. Mercury (0.055 Earth masses, 0.383 radii): g ≈ 3.7 N kg⁻¹. Use the ratio trick to skip huge numbers!

4 · Gravitational Potential & Energy

POTENTIAL Φ (phi)

Gravitational potential at a point = work done per unit mass bringing a test mass from infinity to that point.

Φ = −GM / r   [J kg⁻¹]

Negative because work must be extracted/given as the mass descends to a lower energy.

POTENTIAL ENERGY

Multiply by the mass you move:

Ep = m Φ = −GMm / r

really Joules now. Zero at infinity, decreasing as masses approach.

FIELD vs POTENTIAL

g: force per unit mass, “how hard you are pulled”. Φ: energy per unit mass, “how much deeper you are”. g = −dΦ/dr — the field is the potential gradient.

CHANGES OF ENERGY

To escape from the surface you must supply energy to climb the well: the escape energy per kg is GM/R, so the escape speed satisfies ½v² = GM/R ⇒ v = √(2GM/R). This — not mgΔh — is why lifting a spacecraft out of Earth’s pull can’t be approximated by mgΔh alone.

5 · Escape, Wells, and the Work Consolidation

ENERGY TO LAUNCH

ΔΦ = Φ₂ − Φ₁ = GM(1/r₁ − 1/r₂) has no m

energy needed = mΔΦ (per kg). At infinity Φ = 0 → escape from radius R requires m·GM/R of energy.

THE WELL SHAPE

Deep, narrow wells = heavy planet (mass 2M digs twice as deep). Wide, shallow wells = big planet (radius 2R moves the wall out). Escape: the least energy is needed from the one with the shallower well rise from the starting point.

FALLING ROCK

Rock of mass m falling from rest “far away” onto a planet of radius r and mass M: ΔE = GMm/r = ½mv² ⇒ v = √(2GM/r). Mass cancels — every rock hits at the same speed.

ESCAPE & ASSEMBLY

Escape speed from Earth ≈ 11 km s⁻¹, from v = √(2GM/R). It’s far cheaper to assemble interplanetary ships in orbit than to haul them up from the surface — that’s why the Mars missions stage from Earth orbit. The famous exam line: “less energy to escape from the shallower well”.

6 · Circular Orbits Under Gravity

For a satellite, gravity is the centripetal force. One force, pointing to the centre, at 90° to the velocity.

ORBITAL SPEED — THE MASS CANCELS!

GMm/r² = mv²/r ⇒ v = √(GM/r)

Closer orbits are faster. v doesn’t depend on satellite mass.

equate gravity to centripetal: GMm/r² = mv²/r — satellite mass m cancels
▼
v = √(GM/r); low r → big speed
▼
period T = 2πr/v (distance ÷ speed) → Kepler’s law next page
ISS CHECK

ISS at r = 6.7 × 10⁶ m: v = √(6.67e-11 × 6.0e24 / 6.7e6) ≈ 7.7 km s⁻¹, T ≈ 5500 s ≈ 1.5 hours. Astronauts float (see §9) — gravity never vanishes, they just keep falling.

7 · Kepler’s Third Law: T² ∝ r³

FROM v AND T

v = 2πr/T; square: v² = 4π²r²/T². Equate to GM/r:

T² = (4π²/GM)·r³   ·   T²/r³ = 4π²/GM

constant for all satellites of the same central body (M).

THE DATA FIT (table 13.1)

Mercury: T 0.241 yr, r 57.9e6 km → T²/r³ ≈ 2.99 × 10⁻²⁵. Earth: 1.00/150e6³ ≈ 2.96e-25. The ratio is flat at ~3.0e-25 yr² km⁻³ for every planet — one straight line through the data proves Kepler’s empirical law, later explained by Newton’s inverse-square law.

GRAPH → TWO MASSES

Plot T² against r³: a straight line through the origin with gradient 4π²/GM — so the graph measures the central body’s mass (e.g. Neptune from its moons).

8 · Geostationary & Other Orbits

Geostationary orbit: satellite that stays over the same point of the Equator. Requirements:

  • orbital period = Earth’s rotation = 24 h (86 400 s)
  • orbit in the equatorial plane, motion west → east
  • height fixed by 4π²/(GM) r³ = T² template
THE CLARKE BELT NUMBER

r³ = GMT²/4π² → r = (6.67e-11 × 6.0e24 × 86400² / 4π²)^(1/3)

r ≈ 4.23 × 10⁷ m ≈ 6.6 Earth radii from the centre (≈ 35 900 km above)

→ the “geostationary height” every MCQ remembers.

SIGNAL DELAY

Up to 42 300 km and back ≈ 2 × 4.23×10⁷ m ÷ 3.0×10⁸ m s⁻¹ ≈ 0.28 s round trip. Cables on the ground beat that delay — so most phone traffic stays underground. If the exam asks you to “estimate the delay”, remember c = 3.0×10⁸ m s⁻¹.

9 · Weightlessness — Apparent, Not True

On the ISS, everything — astronaut, pencil, tables — is in perpetual free fall toward Earth while moving sideways fast enough to keep missing it. There is no support force to register as weight.

BALANCED LIFT SCALES

At rest: reading 10g N. Going up at 0.1g ⇒ reading 11g N (feels heavy). Descending at 0.1g ⇒ 9g N. Cable cut ⇒ accelerate at g ⇒ reading zero — you and the pencil fall together, it doesn’t drift to the floor.

FREE-FALL INSIDE THE ISS

Gravity is still fully present at orbital height (g ≈ 8.8 m s⁻² at ISS altitude) — “zero gravity” means zero apparent weight, from acceleration matching the free fall. Real zero gravity happens only far from every mass. Astronauts lose calcium and their muscles weaken, then bounce back when they return.

10 · Worked Examples & Revision

SOLVED · MOON FROM EARTH

Moon r = 3.84×10⁸ m, Mₑ = 6.0×10²⁴ kg: v = √(GM/r) ≈ 1.02 km s⁻¹ ≈ 1 km s⁻¹. T = 2πr/v = 2π×3.84e8/1.02e3 ≈ 2.4×10⁶ s ≈ 27 days ✓

SOLVED · ISS-SCALE ORBIT

Satellite of mass 1.0 kg in orbit at 200 km: r = 6.6×10⁶ m. g = GM/r² = 6.67e-11×6.0e24/(6.6e6)² ≈ 9.17 N kg⁻¹. Weight nearly the same as at sea level (9.81 N) — yet the astronaut on board feels nothing, because they are in free fall.

PAST-PAPER TACTICS
  1. Constancy near ground: r = R ⇒ field g ≈ 9.81 N kg⁻¹; at 2R it’s g/4 ≈ 2.4 N kg⁻¹.
  2. g of a planet = g_E × (M_p/M_E) / (r_p/r_E)² — solve with ratios, not with G.
  3. Reading data (Table 17.1): field at Moon’s orbit: GM/r² ≈ 2.7 × 10⁻³ N kg⁻¹.
FORMULA BANK

F = Gm₁m₂/r²
g = F/m = GM/r²
Φ = −GM/r; E_p = −GMm/r
v = √(GM/r) · T = 2πr/v · T² = (4π²/GM)r³
escape needs m·GM/R

TRAP LIST

r is centre-to-centre, not altitude · minus signs in Φ · g and Φ are different units (N kg⁻¹ vs J kg⁻¹) · “satellite mass cancels” in orbit speed · near-surface g ~ constant, deep-space is GM/r² · don’t use mgΔh for long distances.
