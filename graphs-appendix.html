Graphs Appendix

Every graph below is annotated with the two questions Cambridge always asks: what does the gradient represent? and what does the area represent?

1 · The Golden Rules of Graphs

RULE 1 · GRADIENT

gradient = (quantity on y-axis) ÷ (quantity on x-axis). Example: gradient of a v–t graph = (m s⁻¹)/(s) = m s⁻² = acceleration.

RULE 2 · AREA

area = (y-axis quantity) × (x-axis quantity). Example: area under a v–t graph = (m s⁻¹)(s) = m = displacement.

EXAM TECHNIQUE
  • Always read the axis labels and units first — a common trap is a speed–time graph mistaken for displacement–time.
  • Check for a false origin (y-axis not starting at zero) before reading intercepts.
  • Use a large triangle (more than half the line) when measuring a gradient.
  • Curved line? Draw a tangent for the gradient at a point.

2 · Kinematics Graphs

Displacement–time: gradient = velocity

Velocity–time: gradient = acceleration, area = displacement

SIGN OF AREA

Area above the t-axis = positive displacement; area below = negative. Distance = sum of magnitudes; displacement = signed sum.

3 · Forces & Pressure Graphs

Pressure difference vs depth: gradient = ρg

INTERPRET

Δp = ρgΔh is linear in Δh, so the graph is a straight line through the origin; steeper gradient for a denser liquid.

Moments: the balanced beam

Couple: torque = F × d

Upthrust: pressure difference across an object
