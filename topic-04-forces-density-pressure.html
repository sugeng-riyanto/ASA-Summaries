EXAMINER'S BRIEF

This topic bridges statics (equilibrium, moments) and fluid mechanics (pressure, upthrust). The golden rule for moments: always use the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. For fluids: pressure acts in all directions at a point, and increases with depth.

1 · Overview & Concept Map

Forces can cause translation (linear acceleration) or rotation (turning effect). This topic focuses on the turning effect and the conditions for equilibrium, then extends to forces in fluids.

2 · Centre of Gravity

DEFINITION

The centre of gravity (C.G.) of an object is the point at which the whole weight of the object may be considered to act.

  • For a uniform object (e.g., a ruler), the C.G. is at the geometrical centre.
  • For a sphere, it is at the centre.
  • For a person standing upright, it is roughly in the middle of the body, behind the navel.
  • The C.G. can lie outside the body (e.g., a high-jumper curving over the bar).
PRACTICAL ACTIVITY

To find the C.G. of an irregular lamina: suspend it freely from two or three points. At each suspension, the C.G. lies vertically below the point of suspension (use a plumb line). The C.G. is at the intersection of the lines drawn.

EXAMINER TRAP

Weight always acts vertically downwards through the C.G. When solving moment problems, treat the entire weight as a single force acting at the C.G. — do not try to sum individual parts.

3 · Moment of a Force

KEY EQUATION

Moment of a force = F × d

where d = perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force.

Unit: newton-metre (N m)

SOLVED · MOMENT AT AN ANGLE

A light rod AB of length 0.45 m is held at A, making an angle of 65° to the vertical. A vertical force of 15 N acts at B.

The perpendicular distance from A to the line of action of the force = 0.45 × sin 65° = 0.408 m.

Moment = 15 × 0.408 = 6.1 N m.

TWO METHODS FOR ANGLED FORCES

Method 1: Find the perpendicular distance d = l sin θ, then Moment = F × d.

Method 2: Resolve the force into a component perpendicular to the lever (F sin θ), then Moment = (F sin θ) × l.

Both give the same answer: Moment = F l sin θ.

CRITICAL POINT

Any force whose line of action passes through the pivot has zero moment (d = 0). This is why choosing moments about a pivot with an unknown force is a powerful problem-solving strategy — it eliminates that unknown from the equation.

4 · Couples & Torque

DEFINITION · COUPLE

A couple consists of two forces, equal in magnitude but opposite in direction, whose lines of action do not coincide. A couple produces rotation only — no linear acceleration.

TORQUE OF A COUPLE

Torque = F × d

where d = perpendicular distance between the two forces.

Unit: newton-metre (N m)

SOLVED · TORQUE CALCULATION

Two forces, each of magnitude 30 N, act in opposite directions with their lines of action separated by 25 cm.

Torque = F × d = 30 × 0.25 = 7.5 N m.

COUPLE vs SINGLE FORCE

Single force: moment depends on the chosen pivot. Also tends to cause linear acceleration.

Couple: torque is independent of the chosen point — it depends only on F and d. It causes pure rotation with no translation. This is why a couple is sometimes called a "pure turning effect".

EXAMPLES IN EVERYDAY LIFE
  • Turning a steering wheel with both hands
  • Opening a tap
  • Using a screwdriver
  • Winding up a watch

5 · Principle of Moments

PRINCIPLE OF MOMENTS

For an object in rotational equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments about that same point.

Σ(clockwise moments) = Σ(anticlockwise moments)

SOLVED · SEE-SAW BALANCE

A 20 N force acts 2.0 m from the pivot (anticlockwise). A 40 N force acts 1.0 m from the pivot (clockwise).

Anticlockwise moment = 20 × 2.0 = 40 N m.

Clockwise moment = 40 × 1.0 = 40 N m.

Since 40 = 40, the see-saw is balanced.

SOLVED · HUMAN ARM (BICEPS)

Forearm weight = 15 N (C.G. at 16 cm from elbow). Object in hand = 50 N (35 cm from elbow). Biceps attaches 4.0 cm from elbow.

Clockwise moments = (15 × 0.16) + (50 × 0.35) = 2.4 + 17.5 = 19.9 N m.

Anticlockwise moment = F × 0.04.

F = 19.9 / 0.04 = ≈ 500 N.

The biceps exerts ~10× the weight being lifted — a large mechanical disadvantage!

STRATEGY FOR MOMENT PROBLEMS
  1. Draw a clear diagram showing all forces and distances.
  2. Choose a pivot point. If there is an unknown force at a point, take moments about that point to eliminate it.
  3. Calculate clockwise and anticlockwise moments separately.
  4. Equate them and solve for the unknown.
  5. Check equilibrium of forces if needed (ΣF = 0).

6 · Conditions for Complete Equilibrium

TWO CONDITIONS

For an object to be in complete equilibrium (no translation, no rotation):

  1. Resultant force = 0 (ΣF = 0 in any direction) → no linear acceleration
  2. Resultant moment = 0 (ΣM = 0 about any point) → no angular acceleration
VECTOR TRIANGLE FOR EQUILIBRIUM

When three coplanar forces act on an object in equilibrium, they can be represented by the sides of a closed triangle, drawn head-to-tail in sequence.

If the triangle does not close → there is a resultant force → the object is not in equilibrium.

TWO-WAY USE

Forward: If you calculate the resultant and find it is zero → the object is in equilibrium.

Backward: If you know the object is in equilibrium → the forces must add to zero → use this to find unknown forces.

7 · Density

KEY EQUATION

ρ = m / V

where ρ = density (kg m⁻³), m = mass (kg), V = volume (m³)

MaterialDensity / kg m⁻³
Air (at sea level)1.2
Water1000
Sea water1030
Aluminium2700
Iron / Steel7800
Lead11 400
Gold19 300
SOLVED · DENSITY OF A SPHERE

An iron sphere of radius 0.18 m has mass 190 kg.

V = (4/3)πr³ = (4/3)π(0.18)³ = 0.0244 m³.

ρ = m/V = 190 / 0.0244 = ≈ 7800 kg m⁻³.

USEFUL VOLUME FORMULAE
  • Rectangular block: V = length × width × height
  • Cylinder: V = πr²l
  • Sphere: V = (4/3)πr³
  • For irregular objects: use water displacement in a measuring cylinder.

8 · Pressure

KEY EQUATION

p = F / A

where p = pressure (Pa = N m⁻²), F = force perpendicular to the surface (N), A = area (m²)

  • Pressure is a scalar quantity — it acts equally in all directions at a point in a fluid.
  • The force must be perpendicular to the surface.
  • 1 Pa = 1 N m⁻². Atmospheric pressure ≈ 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa ≈ 101 kPa.
EXAMINER TRAP

Pressure depends on area, not just force. A sharp knife cuts because the same force acts over a much smaller area → much greater pressure. A snowshoe distributes weight over a large area → small pressure → you don't sink.

9 · Hydrostatic Pressure & Derivation of Δp = ρgΔh

DERIVATION

Consider a column of liquid of density ρ, cross-sectional area A, and height h above a point at depth h.

Step 1: Weight of the column: W = mg = ρVg = ρAhg

Step 2: Pressure at depth h due to this column: p = W/A = ρAhg/A = ρgh

Step 3: For two points at depths h₁ and h₂:

Δp = ρgΔh

SOLVED · PRESSURE IN A SWIMMING POOL

Calculate the excess pressure (over atmospheric) at a point 1.2 m below the surface of water (ρ = 1000 kg m⁻³).

p = ρgh = 1000 × 9.81 × 1.2 = 1.2 × 10⁴ Pa.

Total (absolute) pressure = atmospheric + excess = 1.01 × 10⁵ + 1.2 × 10⁴ = 1.13 × 10⁵ Pa.

KEY POINTS ABOUT LIQUID PRESSURE
  • Pressure increases with depth (p = ρgh).
  • Pressure acts in all directions at a point.
  • Pressure is independent of the shape of the container — it depends only on depth and density.
  • If an external pressure (e.g., atmospheric) acts on the surface, the absolute pressure = external pressure + ρgh.

10 · Upthrust & Archimedes' Principle

ORIGIN OF UPTHRUST

An object immersed in a fluid experiences pressure on all sides. Because pressure increases with depth, the pressure on the bottom surface is greater than on the top surface. The difference in these forces gives a net upward force: the upthrust (buoyancy force).

ARCHIMEDES' PRINCIPLE

The upthrust acting on an object immersed in a fluid is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.

F_upthrust = ρfluid × g × Vdisplaced

SOLVED · CYLINDER IN WATER

A metal cylinder of volume 0.50 m³ and density 7800 kg m⁻³ is immersed in water (ρ = 1000 kg m⁻³).

(a) Force to lift in air: Weight = ρmetalgV = 7800 × 9.81 × 0.50 = 3.8 × 10⁴ N.

(b) Force to lift in water: Upthrust = ρwatergV = 1000 × 9.81 × 0.50 = 4.9 × 10³ N.

Force = Weight − Upthrust = 3.8 × 10⁴ − 4.9 × 10³ = 3.3 × 10⁴ N.

FLOATING CONDITION

An object floats when the upthrust equals its weight:

ρfluid × g × Vsubmerged = ρobject × g × Vtotal

This simplifies to: fraction submerged = ρobject / ρfluid.

If ρobject < ρfluid → object floats. If ρobject > ρfluid → object sinks.

EXAMINER TRAP

Upthrust depends on the density of the fluid, not the density of the object. A steel ship floats because it displaces a large volume of water — the average density of the ship (including air inside) is less than water's density.

11 · Worked Examples & Exam Practice

EXAMPLE 1 · BEAM WITH FORCES

A uniform beam of length 0.40 m and weight W is held horizontally. A bar exerts a force of 33 N at 52° to the horizontal. An empty bucket of weight 12 N hangs from the far end. Taking moments about the left end:

Vertical component of bar force = 33 sin 52° = 26 N (upward).

Clockwise moments = W × 0.20 + 12 × 0.40 = 0.20W + 4.8.

Anticlockwise moment = 26 × 0.10 = 2.6 N m. (Bar acts 0.10 m from left end.)

Equating: 0.20W + 4.8 = 2.6 → W = −11 N? This suggests the bar is closer to the right. Re-check geometry: if bar acts at 0.30 m from left: anticlockwise = 26 × 0.30 = 7.8. Then 0.20W + 4.8 = 7.8 → W = 15 N.

(Always sketch carefully and verify distances from the chosen pivot.)

EXAMPLE 2 · FLAGPOLE

A flagpole of mass 25 kg is held horizontally by a cable. The C.G. is 1.5 m from the fixed end. The cable is attached at the far end (3.0 m from the fixed end) at 40° to the horizontal.

Weight = 25 × 9.81 = 245 N.

Taking moments about the fixed end: T sin 40° × 3.0 = 245 × 1.5.

T = (245 × 1.5) / (3.0 × sin 40°) = 367.5 / 1.93 = ≈ 190 N.

EXAMPLE 3 · DENSITY OF A DISC

A cylindrical disc has diameter 28 mm and thickness 12 mm. Density = 6.8 × 10³ kg m⁻³.

V = πr²l = π(0.014)²(0.012) = 7.39 × 10⁻⁶ m³.

m = ρV = 6.8 × 10³ × 7.39 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.050 kg.

Weight = mg = 0.050 × 9.81 = 0.49 N (to 2 s.f.).

EXAMPLE 4 · SPHERE IN SEA WATER

A solid sphere of diameter 30.0 cm is fully immersed in sea water (ρ = 1030 kg m⁻³). Weight of sphere = 1100 N.

(a) V = (4/3)π(0.15)³ = 0.0141 m³. ρ_sphere = m/V = (1100/9.81)/0.0141 = ≈ 7900 kg m⁻³.

(b) Upthrust = ρgV = 1030 × 9.81 × 0.0141 = ≈ 140 N.

(c) Initial resultant force = Weight − Upthrust = 1100 − 140 = 960 N.

Initial acceleration = 960 / (1100/9.81) = 960 / 112 = ≈ 8.6 m s⁻².

TRY YOURSELF (answers only)
  1. A nut is tightened to a torque of 16 N m using a spanner of length 24 cm. Force required = 16/0.24 = 67 N.
  2. A rectangular block of mass 150 kg has sides 60 cm × 90 cm × 12 cm. Minimum pressure on ground = weight / largest area = (150 × 9.81) / (0.60 × 0.90) = 2.7 kPa.
  3. A metal drum of volume 0.500 m³ and weight 800 N is held 300 m below the sea surface (ρ = 1030 kg m⁻³). Upthrust = 1030 × 9.81 × 0.500 = 5050 N. Tension in cable = Upthrust − Weight = 5050 − 800 = 4.3 kN (drum is buoyant, cable holds it down).

12 · Mistakes · Tips · Tricks · One-Page Revision

✗ Mistake✓ Correction
Using the distance along the lever instead of the perpendicular distance.Always find the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. If the force is at angle θ to the lever, use d = l sin θ.
Confusing moment of a force with torque of a couple.Moment of a force depends on the pivot. Torque of a couple is independent of the pivot (depends only on F and d between forces).
Forgetting to include the weight of a uniform beam acting at its centre.For a uniform beam, weight acts at the geometrical centre. Include it in moment calculations.
Using p = F/A with a non-perpendicular force.F must be the component perpendicular to the surface.
Confusing gauge pressure with absolute pressure.Gauge pressure = ρgh. Absolute pressure = atmospheric + ρgh. Read the question carefully.
Thinking upthrust depends on the object's density.Upthrust = ρfluidgVdisplaced. It depends on the fluid's density, not the object's.
MEMORY TRICKS
  • Moment: "Force × Perpendicular Distance" — FPD.
  • Couple: "Two equal, opposite, parallel forces → pure rotation."
  • Equilibrium: "No resultant force AND no resultant moment."
  • Pressure in liquid: "ρgh — density, gravity, height."
  • Archimedes: "Upthrust = weight of fluid displaced."
  • Floating: "If object density < fluid density → it floats."
