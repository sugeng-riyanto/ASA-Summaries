Capacitance

C = Q/VW = ½QVW = ½CV²series / parallelτ = RCexponential decay
EXAMINER'S BRIEF

Capacitance is the electrostatics chapter, and the examiners reward two skills above all: the area-under-the-graph argument that energy = ½QV (a triangle under the Q–V line), and the exponential discharge Q = Q₀e^(−t/RC). Capacitors in series is where most people invert — the smaller result is correct! Get τ = RC and the “each series capacitor holds the same Q” idea nailed, and Paper 4 washes out.

1 · Capacitors

CONSTRUCTION

Two metal plates separated by an insulator (dielectric). Connect to a supply: electrons pile onto one plate (−Q), the other plate is left +Q. The capacitor stores charge — equal and opposite on the two plates.

Charging stops when the p.d. across the capacitor equals the battery e.m.f.

TOTAL CHARGE = ZERO!

Overall the two plates are neutral — “stored charge Q” means the magnitude on one plate. Scale it: 1 pF = 10⁻¹² F, 1 nF = 10⁻⁹ F, 1 µF = 10⁻⁶ F.

2 · Capacitance — C = Q/V

DEFINITION

Capacitance = the charge stored per unit potential difference across it.

C = Q/V   [C] = F = C V⁻¹

bigger plates −→ more charge at the same V −→ bigger C

CALC

Charge a 220 µF cap to 15 V → Q = VC = 15 × 220 × 10⁻⁶ = 3.3 mC. Or: 0.033 C on a 2200 µF cap → V = Q/C = 15 V.

3 · Energy Stored in a Capacitor

To push a total charge Q onto a plate, work = area under the p.d.–charge graph (a straight line through the origin) → a triangle.

ENERGY

W = ½QV = ½CV² = ½Q²/C

W ∝ V² — double the charging voltage, quadruple the stored energy!

WORKED 1

2000 µF to 10 V → W = ½ × 2000×10⁻⁶ × 100 = 0.10 J. Compare: rechargeable battery at 10 000 J — capacitors are for bursts, not long-term.

WHY NOT W = QV?

First-charge electrons feel little repulsion; later ones fight a crowded plate. Average pushing p.d. is ½V — hence the half. (That simple “½” is a classic test of the graph-nerve.)

4 · Capacitors in Parallel

FORMULA + DERIVATION

Same p.d. V across each; charges add:

Ctotal = C₁ + C₂ + C₃ + …

Qtot = C₁V + C₂V = (C₁ + C₂)V ⇒ compare with Q = CV

SAID AS

Parallel capacitors = bigger plate = more charge stored for a given V. Add directly.

FAST CHECK

Two 100 µF in parallel → 200 µF. Charged to 20 V: Q = CV = 200 × 20 = 4000 µC.

5 · Capacitors in Series

SERIES (shares charge, splits volts)

1/Ctotal = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + 1/C₃ + …

both hold the same charge Q; the p.d.s add: V = V₁ + V₂

WHY SAME Q?

−Q lands on the plate of C₁; it repels −Q off the other plate on; that −Q streams on C₂'s left plate; who in turn pushes +Q out to C₂'s right. So Q is identical throughout the chain — conservation of charge! The middle section stays neutral.

WORKED 2

300 µF and 600 µF in series: 1/C = 1/300 + 1/600 = 1/200 ⇒ C = 200 µF.
Notice it's less than either — series shrinks capacitance, exactly like resistors (in that logic).

THE INVERTED TWIN

Series resistors & parallel capacitors both use reciprocals. Capacitors: parallel ⇒ straight sum, series ⇒ reciprocal. Don't flip them!

6 · Capacitor Networks

① All in series: add reciprocals.
▼
② All in parallel: add plainly.
▼
③ Two parallel + one series: solve the pair parallel first, then that result in series with the third.
▼
④ Two series + one parallel: pair series first, then add the third in parallel.
SAME ORDER AS RESISTORS

The solving sequence is identical to resistor networks — collect the easy group, then combine with the odd one out.

7 · Sharing Charge & Energy

CONNECT TWO CAPACITORS

Charge is conserved: the total Q is shared across the joined pair, so:

Ctot = C₁ + C₂ · Qfinal = Qoriginal · V = Qfinal/Ctot

Wfinal = ½CtotV² < Winitial — some energy leaks as heat!

WORKED 3

100 mF charged to 10 V then connected to another 100 mF:
Q = 1.0 C, Ctot = 200 mF, V = 5.0 V; W = ½×0.2×25 = 2.5 J vs 5.0 J at start → half the energy dissipated along the wires.

8 · Capacitance of a Isolated Sphere

SPHERE CAPACITY

For a conducting sphere of radius r: V = Q/(4πε₀r), so

C = 4πε₀r

Earth: C ≈ 4πε₀ × 6.4×10⁶ ≈ ~7×10⁻⁴ F — that's a whole farad's worth, surprisingly!

9 · Charge & Discharge of Capacitors

Connect to P to charge, to Q to discharge through R. Both current and p.d. collapse exponentially — the same shape as radioactive decay!

EXPONENTIAL DOOM CLUB

charge, p.d., and current all decay with the same law:

Q = Q₀e⁻ᵗ/RC    V = V₀e⁻ᵗ/RC    I = I₀e⁻ᵗ/RC

More R (slower drain) · more C (more charge) ⇒ both spread the decay out

WHY IT DECAYS

As charge leaves, V falls → smaller RESISTOR current → slower drain → exponential. Just like radioactive atoms: fewer parents left ⇒ fewer decays each blink.

10 · Time Constant & Exam Skills

TIME CONSTANT

τ = RC

[RC] = Ω·F = s ✓ · after one τ, values fall to e⁻¹ ≈ 0.37 = 37% of start

RULES OF THumb

At t = τ·1: ~63% gone · after ~5τ you treat it as fully discharged (under 1%).

WORKED 4

V₀ = 240 V, C = 500 µF, R = 600 Ω: I₀ = 240/600 = 0.40 A, τ = 300×10⁻³ s.
Time to reach I = 0.10 A: 0.10 = 0.40e⁻ᵗ/τ ⇒ (t/τ) = ln 4 ⇒ t = 0.3 × 1.386 ≈ 0.42 s.

FORMULA BANK

C=Q/V · W = ½QV = ½CV² = ½Q²/C
parallel: C = ΣC
series: 1/C = Σ1/Ci
C_sphere = 4πε₀r
τ = RC · Q=Q₀e⁻ᵗ/τ

TRAP LIST

✗ ½ missing in energy ✗ inverting series rule — series makes C SMALLER ✗ mixing W=½QV with QV ✗ °C → K not relevant but units of τ still – F·Ω → s; keep everything in base units!

Charge it, hold it, dump it — energy is never even. ✔
