EXAMINER’S BRIEF

Everything here starts from pressure = molecular collisions. You must be able to state the four assumptions of kinetic theory, derive pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ from a single molecule in a cube, and then equate it to pV = NkT to pull out ⟨Ek⟩ = ¾kT. Always convert °C → K before substituting. R = 8.31 J K⁻¹mol⁻¹, k = R/NA = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹.

1 · The Mole & Avogadro’s Constant

Amount of substance is an SI base quantity with base unit the mole (mol). One mole of any substance contains NA particles.

AVOGADRO

NA = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹

Number of particles = moles × NA  ·  moles = mass / molar mass

WORKED · NEON

Molar mass of neon 20.0 g → 2.5 g contains: 2.5/20.0 × 6.02×10²³ = 7.5 × 10²² atoms.

MOLECULES vs ATOMS — TRAP

1 mol of O2 molecules holds 2 mol of O atoms. 1.51 × 10²⁴ O2 molecules = 2.51 mol; mass = 2.51 × 32 = 80.3 g.

QUICK ESTIMATE

1.0 kg of ordinary matter contains roughly 10²⁶ atoms — test it: (1.0 kg)/(27 g/mol for Al ≈ 0.027 kg/mol) × 6.02×10²³ ≈ 2.2 × 10²⁵.

ABSOLUTE ZERO ORIGIN

Cooling a gas at constant pressure shrinks its volume. Projecting the V–θ graph back, all gases meet the axis at −273 °C = 0 K — this is historically how absolute zero was discovered.

3 · Boyle’s Law & Charles’s Law

BOYLE (1662)

Fixed mass, constant temperature:

pV = constant   ·   p₁V₁ = p₂V₂

Why? Halve V → double molecules per m³ → twice collisions per second.

CHARLES & GAY-LUSSAC

Constant pressure:  V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

Constant volume:  p₁/T₁ = p₂/T₂

COMBINED

pV/T = constant  ·  p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂

REAL GAS WARNING

Boyle/Charles are only accurate for real gases at moderate pressures and well above the liquefaction temperature. Below these they fail dramatically — witness nitrogen condensing to liquid at 77 K.

4 · Real vs Ideal Gases

DEFINITION

An ideal gas is one that obeys the equation of state

pV ∝ T   (with the thermodynamic temperature T)

at all pressures, volumes and temperatures. Real gases approach this at low pressure and high temperature.

WHY REAL GASES DEVIATE

At very high pressure or low temperature the molecules get close enough that electrical forces between them become significant, and the molecules’ own volume stops being negligible. Compression then needs more pressure than pV = nRT predicts.

STP MOLE VOLUME

Volume of 1 mole at 273 K, 1.01×10⁵ Pa: V = nRT/p = (1 × 8.31 × 273)/(1.01×10⁵) = 2.35 × 10⁻² m³ (a cube of side ~28 cm). Number density ≈ 2.7 × 10²⁵ molecules per m³.

5 · Equation of State pV = nRT = NkT

THE TWO FORMS

pV = nRT   ·   pV = NkT

R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ (same for every gas!)  ·  k = R/NA = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹

SymbolMeaning
namount of gas in moles
Nnumber of molecules
Rmolar (universal) gas constant, 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹
kBoltzmann constant, 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ = R/NA
WORKED · CAR TYRE

0.020 m³ air at 27 °C (300 K), 3.0×10⁵ Pa, molar mass 28.8 g/mol: n = pV/RT = 3.0×10⁵ × 0.020/(8.31×300) = 2.41 mol → mass = 2.41 × 0.0288 = 69 g.

THE SIGNATURE ERROR

Every year students forget to convert °C → K. 20 °C is 293 K. Also, use Pa for p, m³ for V, and moles — never grams — for n.

6 · Kinetic Theory — the Four Assumptions

The kinetic theory links microscopic molecular motion (speed, mass) to the macroscopic quantities p, V, T. Learn these four assumptions word for word — Cambridge loves asking for them.

AssumptionWhy it matters
1. A gas contains a very large number of molecules moving at random, colliding elastically with the walls and each other.No kinetic energy is lost; we can average over many molecules.
2. The volume of the molecules is negligible compared with the volume of the container.Almost all the box is empty space.
3. The forces between molecules are negligible except during collisions.Otherwise molecules would clump.
4. The collision time is negligible compared with the time between collisions.Molecules spend almost all their time moving freely.
THINK OF IT TOO

Internal energy of an ideal gas = total kinetic energy of its molecules (no potential term — no forces between molecules!). This one fact sets up all of Topic 17.

7 · Deriving pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩

Follow the five steps; you must be able to reproduce this proof.

1 · Elastic wall hit: velocity cₓ → −cₓ, so Δp = 2mcₓ.
▼
2 · Time between hits on one face = 2L/cₓ (round trip).
▼
3 · Rate of change = 2mcₓ ÷ (2L/cₓ) = mcₓ²/L → force of 1 molecule.
▼
4 · All N molecules → total force Nm⟨cₓ²⟩/L, pressure = force/Area = Nm⟨cₓ²⟩/L³ = Nm⟨cₓ²⟩/V.
▼
5 · 3D: c² = cₓ² + cᵧ² + cz² and ⟨cₓ²⟩ = ⅓⟨c²⟩.
THE RESULT

pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩   ·   p = ⅓ρ⟨c²⟩

macroscopic (p, V) on one side; microscopic (m, ⟨c²⟩) on the other — the bridge!

PLAUSIBLE CHECK

Pressure ↑ with more molecules, heavier molecules, faster molecules; pressure ↓ as V grows. That is Boyle’s law revealed by the model.

8 · Temperature & Mean Kinetic Energy

Now equate the two equations for pV:

THE KILLER LINK

⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ = NkT  ⇒  ½m⟨c²⟩ = ¾kT

mean translational kinetic energy of a molecule ∝ T

So: T (K) is proportional to the average translational k.e. of a molecule.

Average energy per molecule (monatomic gas) = ¾kT

IdeaResult
Same temperature for a mixtureN₂, O₂, CO₂ all have the same mean k.e.; heavier ones move slower.
Double T (300 → 600 K)mean k.e. doubles, mean speed × √2 (since k.e. ∝ v²).
Monatomic vs diatomicmonatomic: translational only; diatomic can also rotate.
REMEMBER AS “¾ kT”

“Half of three-halves” — the rhymed memory line from the textbook: average k.e. is three-halves kT. For 1 mole: total internal energy = ¾ R T (so ΔU = ¾RΔT always).

9 · Root-Mean-Square Speed

DEFINITION

cr.m.s. = √⟨c²⟩ = √(3kT/m)= √(3RT/M)

root of the mean of the squares — the “characteristic” speed used to compute kinetic energy.

WORKSHEET · 4 speeds

200, 400, 600, 800 m/s: mean ⟨c⟩ = 500; ⟨c²⟩ = (40000+160000+360000+640000)/4 = 300000 m²/s²; cr.m.s. = √300000 ≈ 548 m/s.

vs MEAN SPEED

⟨c²⟩ ≠ (⟨c⟩)² in general. r.m.s. speed is the square root of the mean-square; it is a little larger than the mean speed (mean ≈ 0.92 × r.m.s.).

HINT AIR AT STP

Air near STP: ~400 m/s molecules. Uses fast escape from Earth’s gravity ~11 km/s — fine, unlike hydrogen, which leaks away.

10 · Worked Examples & Revision

EXAMPLE A · r.m.s. speed

N₂ molecule m = 4.6×10⁻²⁶ kg at 27 °C (300 K): cr.m.s. = √(3×1.38×10⁻²³×300/4.6×10⁻²⁶) = 520 m/s.

EXAMPLE B · solar helium

Helium m = 6.6×10⁻²⁷ kg, 6000 K: cr.m.s. = √(3×1.38×10⁻²³×6000/6.6×10⁻²⁷) ≈ 6100 m/s.

EXAMPLE C · balloon rise

Ground 17 °C, 1.0 atm → 2.5 km: 5 °C, 0.75 atm: V₂/V₁ = (p₁/p₂)(T₂/T₁) = (1/0.75)×(278/290) ≈ 1.28.
