EXAMINER'S BRIEF

This topic recycles earlier ideas in new clothes — exponential decay (from nuclear physics), the wave equation (ultrasound), photons and annihilation (quantum physics). The two exam formulas you must own: X-ray/ultrasound attenuation I = I₀e^(−μx) and the reflected fraction I_R/I₀ = ((Z₂−Z₁)/(Z₂+Z₁))².

1 · X-Rays: Production

X-rays are high-frequency, short-wavelength electromagnetic radiation (λ ~ 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻¹³ m). They are produced when fast electrons are decelerated — braking radiation ("Bremsstrahlung"); γ-rays differ only in origin (radioactive decay).

THE X-RAY TUBE
  • Cathode: heated filament emits electrons (thermionic emission).
  • Anode: rotating tungsten disc — electrons accelerate across a p.d. up to 200 kV and slam in, decelerating and radiating X-ray photons.
  • Only ~1% of electron energy becomes X-rays; the rest heats the anode (why it rotates).
  • A thin window lets a collimated beam out; metal tubes collimate it further.
MAXIMUM ENERGY

Electron energy eV = maximum photon energy hf_max:

λ₀ = hc / eV

λ₀ = minimum (cut-off) wavelength — set by the accelerating p.d. V

Increase V → shorter λ₀ → harder (more penetrating) X-rays. The spectrum also has sharp "characteristic" peaks, from energy-level transitions in the target metal.

DOSE CONTROL

The long-wavelength (soft) X-rays are filtered out with an aluminium absorber — otherwise they'd add to the patient's dose without reaching the film. Intensity is controlled by the tube current (more electrons per second → more photons).

2 · Attenuation: I = I₀e^(−μx)

A collimated beam loses intensity as it passes through matter — X-rays ionise atoms and hand over energy. The drop is exponential, exactly like nuclear decay.

ATTENUATION LAW

I = I₀e^(−μx)

I₀ = initial intensity · I = transmitted · x = thickness · μ = linear attenuation coefficient (m⁻¹, cm⁻¹)

μ depends on the material and the photon energy. It is analogous to λ the decay constant. An image of μ through the body IS the X-ray picture.

HALF-THICKNESS x½

The thickness that halves the intensity: x½ = ln 2 / μ. Same relation as half-life. Bone absorbs more than muscle, so it looks white on film — little radiation reaches the detector behind it.

3 · Improving X-ray Images

CONTRAST & DOSAGE
  • Bone vs soft tissue: high contrast automatically (μ_bone ≫ μ_muscle, Z ~ 14 vs ~7).
  • Soft tissue vs soft tissue: poor contrast — uses contrast media (barium meal, iodine injections) — high-Z absorbers that delineate organ edges.
  • Patient dose: reduce exposure using image intensifiers — a phosphor screen + photocathode converts each X-ray photon into thousands of light photons before it hits film, cutting exposure 100–500×.

4 · CT Scanning

THE PRINCIPLE

A conventional X-ray is a 2-D shadow with structures superimposed. A CT scanner (Computed Axial Tomography / CAT) removes the ambiguity: the X-ray tube rotates around the patient in a ring as detectors, and software reconstructs slice-by-slice cross-sections ("tomos" = slice).

HOW IT WORKS
  • The tube emits a fan-shaped beam through many angles.
  • Detector readings for each slice go to a computer, which solves for each small voxel's attenuation (pixel intensities).
  • Stacked slices rebuild a 3-D volume that can be viewed from any angle or re-sliced.
ADVANTAGES

3-D relationships + tissues of similar density distinguished — precise tumour localisation for radio-treatment. But: dose is much higher than a single X-ray.

5 · Ultrasound: Piezo-Electric Transducers

WHAT IS ULTRASOUND?

Longitudinal pressure waves above 20 kHz (medical use ~1–10 MHz). Sound needs matter, so it bounces off/reflected at tissue boundaries. For detail, higher frequencies → shorter λ → better spatial resolution, but they get absorbed sooner.

PIEZO-ELECTRIC EFFECT

A piezo-electric crystal (quartz, PVdFF) changes shape when a p.d. is applied — the reverse works: a stress on the crystal generates an e.m.f. Hence a single transducer both sends (alternating voltage drives the crystal) and receives (echoes stress the crystal) ultrasound. It's tuned to ~λ/2 of the ultrasound; a backing material rapidly damps it after each pulse.

6 · Acoustic Impedance & Reflection

ACOUSTIC IMPEDANCE

Z = ρc

ρ = density (kg m⁻³), c = speed of sound in the material (m s⁻¹)

Unit: kg m⁻² s⁻¹. For air ~430, water ~1.5×10⁶, soft tissue ~1.6×10⁶, bone ~6.4×10⁶.

INTENSITY REFLECTION COEFFICIENT

I_R/I₀ = ((Z₂ − Z₁)/(Z₂ + Z₁))²

at normal incidence to a boundary · the bigger the Z mismatch, the more echo

AIR = DISASTER

Z_air small ≪ Z_skin, so 99.9% of the ultrasound bounces back before entering the body. The coupling gel removes the air layer — it's matched to skin's Z, so the beam gets in. That's why technicians smother the probe with gel.

7 · A-Scan & B-Scan

A-SCAN

One pulse travels in, echoes return from each boundary. Display: spikes vs time — position of each spike gives depth (time × c/2), height gives signal strength. Used for one-dimensional measurements (e.g. eye length).

B-SCAN

Lots of A-scan lines swept across you become dots — position in x and y plus brightness give a 2-D cross-section, each bright spot from a stronger echo. The transducer moves over the body; the computer rebuilds the image.

DEPTH RULE

Ultrasound takes a "round trip": depth × 2 in the time. Thickness of a slab = (c × Δt)/2.

8 · PET Scanning

THE CONCEPT

Positron Emission Tomography injects a tracer (a glucose-based molecule, FDG, carrying the short-lived β⁺-emitter fluorine-18) that the tissue absorbs. Cancer makes it glow — it eats more glucose.

① Fluorine-18 β⁺ decays: ¹⁸₉F → ¹⁸₈O + e⁺ + ν
▼
② The positron travels <1 mm, then annihilates with an electron: mass → 2 γ-photons flying in opposite directions (momentum conservation).
▼
③ Ring of γ-detectors around the body — a pair of simultaneous detections map a line of response back to the source.
ANNIHILATION ENERGY

Both mass–energy and momentum are conserved. Each γ photon carries ~0.511 MeV (from E = Δmc² for the electron mass; E = hf):

e⁻ + e⁺ → 2γ     E_each ≈ 0.51 MeV

9 · MRI

MAGNETIC RESONANCE IMAGING

MRI (from the resonance idea in Topic 18) exploits nuclear resonance in a strong external magnetic field: hydrogen nuclei in body tissue have a nuclear magnetic state, precession at a resonance frequency proportional to field strength (the Larmor frequency ω₀ = γB).

① Patient sits in a strong static magnetic field; nuclei (mostly ¹H in water/fat) align and precess.
▼
② Radio-frequency pulse of magnetic energy is applied — nuclei absorb the photon, flipping (resonance).
▼
③ Nuclei relax, re-emitting radio waves that are detected; the field gradients encode this position, and a computer reconstructs a vivid 3-D soft-tissue image.
WHY MRI IS GOLD FOR SOFT TISSUE

Unlike X-rays and ultrasound, MRI image contrast comes from the different proton densities / magnetic environments in soft tissue — so a standard with excellent soft-tissue detail, particularly the brain, without ionising radiation.

10 · Worked Examples

X-RAY PHOTON

X-ray tube at V = 30 kV: λ₀ = hc/(eV) = (6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸)/(1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 30×10³) ≈ 4.1×10⁻¹¹ m.

ATTENUATION

Bone μ = 600 m⁻¹, 20 W m⁻² through 4.0 mm: I = 20 e^(−600×0.004) = 20 e^(−2.4) ≈ 1.8 W m⁻² — only ~9% gets through.

ULTRASOUND REFLECTION

Muscle Z₁ = 1.71×10⁶, bone Z₂ = 6.4×10⁶: I_R/I₀ = ((6.4−1.71)/(6.4+1.71))² = (4.69/8.11)² ≈ 0.33 (33%) echoed at the muscle-bone boundary.

ANNIHILATION

e⁻+e⁺ → 2γ: each photon energy = m_e c² = 9.11×10⁻³¹ × (3×10⁸)² ≈ 8.2×10⁻¹⁴ J ≈ 0.511 MeV, frequency E/h ≈ 1.24×10²⁰ Hz.
