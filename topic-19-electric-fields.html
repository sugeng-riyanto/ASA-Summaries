Electric Fields & Coulomb's Law

E = F/QE = V/dCoulomb's lawE = Q/4πε₀r²Potential V−gradient
EXAMINER'S BRIEF

Electric fields run on exactly the same machinery as gravitational fields — so learn the parallel table once and earn twice: force, field strength, potential, and the inverse-square laws all line up. The unique electric twist is the sign: two kinds of charge mean attraction AND repulsion, and potential can be + (near + charge) or − (near − charge). Parallel plates (E = V/d) and the parabolic deflection of electrons are the paper's favourites.

1 · The Concept of an Electric Field

WHAT IT IS

An electric field is a field of force: it exists at any point where a charge experiences an electric force.

Unbalanced charges create it · another charge placed in it feels the pull/push

  • Only electrons can move between and within materials — protons are locked in the nucleus.
  • Like charges repel, opposite charges attract (observable macroscopically).
  • A charge can attract an uncharged object by electrostatic induction (charges redistribute).
FIELD LINE RULES
  • Direction of the arrow = force on a positive test charge.
  • Closer lines = stronger field.
  • Field lines run + to − (or + to Earth's symbol = 0 V).
  • Representations: uniform (parallel plates), radial (point or spherical charge), sphere→plate.

2 · Electric Field Strength

DEFINITION

The electric field strength at a point = the force per unit positive charge on a stationary positive test charge at that point.

E = F/Q   [E] = N C⁻¹

E is a vector — direction = force on the positive charge!

UNITS ONION

E can be quoted as N C⁻¹ OR V m⁻¹ — they're identical. Example: a force of 150 N acts vertically downwards on a 20 mC charge → E = 150/0.020 = 7500 N C⁻¹ downward.

3 · The Uniform Field Between Parallel Plates

KEY FORMULA

E = V/d

V = p.d. between plates · d = plate separation

Derivation: work pulling charge Q a distance d against force E·Q…

Work done by field = F × d = EQ·d
▼
Energy transformed = V × Q
▼
Set equal: EQd = VQ ⇒ E = V/d
WORKED 21.2

Plates 2.0 cm apart, V = 5.0 kV: E = 5000/0.020 = 2.5×10⁵ V m⁻¹.
Force on a dust particle of charge 8.0×10⁻¹⁹ C: F = EQ = 2.5×10⁵ × 8.0×10⁻¹⁹ = 2.0×10⁻¹³ N.

UNITS!

Convert cm → m before dividing. (40 cm ⇒ 0.40 m.)

4 · Force on a Charge & Parabolic Paths

Combining E = F/Q with E = V/d gives the force on any charge in a uniform field:

FORCE ON A CHARGE

F = EQ = QV/d

independent of position — same magnitude & direction everywhere

PATH OF AN ELECTRON

Electron entering between plates perpendicular to the field: horizontal velocity keeps constant (no horizontal force), while a steady vertical electric force accelerates it — a parabola, just like horizontal projectile motion. Gravity is negligible vs the electric force.

SIGN DECISION

Upper plate negative, electron (charges −e) → attracted upwards. A proton (+e) would deflect downwards! Always think: “opposite attracts”.

5 · Coulomb's Law

STATEMENT

Two point charges exert an electric force that's proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

F = (Q₁Q₂) / (4πε₀ r²)

ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻¹² F m⁻¹  ·  r = centre-to-centre distance

  • Inverse-square: double r → quarter F.
  • Like charges: F positive → repulsion. Unlike charges: F negative → attraction.
  • Works for point charges AND uniform spheres (charge acts as if at the centre).
  • By Newton's third law each charge feels an equal-but-opposite force.
TYPICAL NUMBERS

Two charges +2.0 µC and −1.0 µC, centres 10 cm apart but spheres radius 20 cm… centre to centre distance = 20+20+10 = 50 cm = 0.50 m. F = (9×10⁹)(2×10⁻⁶)(1×10⁻⁶)/(0.50)² ≈ 0.072 N attraction.

6 · Field of a Point Charge

Put a small test charge q₂ at distance r from Q₁, use Coulomb's law, then divide by q₂:

RADIAL FIELD

E = Q / (4πε₀r²) = kQ/r²

outside a uniformly charged sphere, the charge considers to be at its centre

E & r

Inverse square: at 2r the value is quarter of that at r; at 3r, one-ninth. (So at 30 cm vs 20 cm → ×(20/30)² = 4/9.)

7 · Electric Potential

DEFINITION

The electric potential at a point = the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a charge from infinity to that point.

Zero at infinity · scalar · add potentials from multiple charges (with their signs)

POINT CHARGE

V = Q / (4πε₀ r)

No minus needed — the sign comes from the charge itself!

  • Uniform field: V rises linearly between plates (a straight line on a V–distance graph).
  • Work done to move a charge between two points: W = V·Q (this is how voltage = J/C is born!).
  • Equipotentials near a point charge get packed tighter close in — like contour lines on a map.
α → GOLD NUCLEI

α-particle (2e) approaches a gold nucleus (79e), stops at 4.5×10⁻¹⁴ m:
Eₚ = (2e)(79e)/(4πε₀ r) with r=4.5×10⁻¹⁴ = ~4×10⁻¹³ J.

8 · Field Strength = −Potential Gradient

THE RELATION

E = −dV/dr   ·   E = −ΔV/Δs

uniform field: E = −V/d (gradient is constant)

TANGENT METHOD (EXAM)

From a V–r graph, draw the tangent at the point (e.g. r = 5 cm), find its gradient, flip the sign → E at that point. If the slope is −1.0×10⁵ V/m, then E = +1.0×10⁵ V m⁻¹.

HILL/WELL PICTURE

Potential hill (near a + charge): a positive test charge rolls “downhill”; the field strength is the steepness of the hill. A − charge makes a well.

WHY ELECTRIC POTENTIAL IS ±

Gravity has only attraction ⇒ potential always negative. Electricity has both signs ⇒ + charge makes + potential, − charge makes − potential.

10 · Worked Examples & Formula Sheet

EXAMPLE · FIELD changing

A parallel-plate potential V is doubled and plate separation cut to ⅓ → new E = (2V)/(d/3) = 6 × Eold. So field strength grows ×6.

EXAMPLE · SPARK PLUG

Breakdown at 5.0×10⁶ V m⁻¹ over a 1.25×10⁻³ m gap → V = Ed = 5.0×10⁶ × 1.25×10⁻³ = 6.3 kV minimum.
