Alternating Currents

I = I₀ sin ωtω = 2πfI_rms = I₀/√2V_rms = V₀/√2P = I²Rrectification + smoothing
EXAMINER'S BRIEF

The r.m.s. idea is the gateway to every mark on this paper: r.m.s. is the steady current that produces the same average power as the a.c. So V_rms = V₀/√2 only for a sine wave, and — the classic trap — mean power = P_max/2, and if you plug V₀ into P = V²/R you double it. Keep your calculator in RAD mode.

1 · Sinusoidal Current

An alternating current reverses direction every half cycle — the electrons slosh back and forth like s.h.m. The current graph is a sine wave.

AC EQUATION

I = I₀ sin ωt

I₀ = peak (max) current · ω = angular frequency = 2πf (rad s⁻¹)

period T = 1/f. Wave crests + troughs are the halves; current is constantly changing, positive then negative.

CALCULATOR!

Set your calculator to radian mode before = I₀ sin ωt. A classic past-paper killer: think ωt is in degrees.

WORKED

I = 5.0 sin (120πt): I₀ = 5.0 A, ω = 120π rad s⁻¹, f = 60 Hz, T = 1/60 s = 16.7 ms.

2 · Alternating Voltage & the CRO

The generator e.m.f. also swings sinusoidally: V = V₀ sin ωt. An oscilloscope draws it out — Y-axis is voltage, X-axis is time.

CRO READING
  • Y-gain setting = volts per centimetre (or division) → amplitude = peak voltage = cm × V/cm.
  • Time-base = time per centimetre → period = cm × time-base → T; frequency f = 1/T.
WORKED

Trace 2 cm tall at Y-gain 2 V/cm → V₀ = 4 V. Period 4 cm at time-base 5 ms/cm → T = 20 ms, f = 50 Hz.

3 · r.m.s. Values

DEFINITION

The r.m.s. value of an a.c. is that steady current (or voltage) which delivers the same average power to a resistive load as the a.c.

I_rms = I₀/√2 = 0.707 I₀    V_rms = V₀/√2 = 0.707 V₀

only for a sinusoidal a.c.

WHY NOT ½?

You might guess ~half the peak, but because the sine dwells near its maximum, 0.707 is the correct figure — the shape beats the intuition.

4 · Power & the Factor of 2

POWER WITH A.C.

Use r.m.s. in the d.c. power equations:

P = V_rms I_rms = I_rms² R = V_rms² / R

average power = ½ × peak power (P_max = V₀I₀)

THE FAMOUS DOUBLE TRAP

Plug the peak V₀ into P = V²/R and you get twice the correct answer — because (V₀/√2)² = V₀²/2. Mean power = half the maximum power for a sinusoidal a.c. — always remember the ½.

WORKED

Resistor R = 20 Ω, V₀ = 25 V → V_rms = 25/√2 = 17.7 V → P = 17.7²/20 = 15.6 W (≈ half of 31.2 W peak).

5 · Why r.m.s. Works

① Square the current: I² is always ≥ 0, pulsating at double the frequency.
▼
② Average it: for a sine, ⟨I²⟩ = ½ I₀².
▼
③ Take the square root → the root of the mean of the square → I_rms = I₀/√2.
WHY P ∝ I²

P = I²R, power follows the square of the current — hence the ½ and the name r.m.s.

6 · Half-Wave Rectification

THE CIRCUIT

One diode in series with the load (resistor R). Diodes only conduct in the forward direction.

OUTPUT

Positive half-cycles pass; negative ones are chopped off. V_out = 0 for half the time.

power available is reduced — that's the downside

7 · Bridge (Full-Wave) Rectifier

FOUR DIODES

The bridge sends both halves the same way through the load:

  • Positive half: A +ve, B −ve → diodes 2 and 3 conduct, 1 and 4 block → current down through R.
  • Negative half: B +ve, A −ve → diodes 4 and 1 conduct → current STILL down through R.

Output current always in the same direction ⇛ full-wave rectified

MEMORISE

Every half cycle, the two opposite diodes conduct in turn; the load never sees a zero-length gap at mains frequency.

8 · Smoothing with a Capacitor

THE FIX

Put a big capacitor C in parallel with the load R. It charges at each peak, then discharges a little between the peaks — smoothing the ripples.

CONTROL THE RIPPLE

Ripple ↓ if you increase the time constant:

τ = CR

larger C · larger R → slower discharge → flatter output

Ideal: τ should be much larger than the time between adjacent peaks (i.e. much more than half a period for a full-wave supply).

9 · Worked Examples

WHICH CAPACITOR?

Half-wave rectifier, R = 1.2 kΩ, peaks 40 ms apart. With C = 10 pF: τ = 10×10⁻⁹ × 1.2×10³ = 1.2×10⁻⁵ s = 0.012 ms — tiny, the cap drains instantly, no smoothing → unsuitable. With C = 500 µF: τ = 500×10⁻⁶ × 1.2×10³ = 0.60 s = 600 ms ≫ 40 ms → good smoothing.
