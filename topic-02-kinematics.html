2 · Scalars vs Vectors

DEFINITIONS

A scalar has magnitude only. A vector has magnitude and direction.

Scalar (size only)Vector (size + direction)
distance, speed, time, mass, density, volume, energy, work, pressuredisplacement, velocity, acceleration, force, weight, momentum
EXAMINER’S TEST“If I reverse the direction, does the quantity change?” Time: 3 min north + 3 min south = 6 min → scalar. Two equal opposite forces cancel → force is a vector.

Resolving into perpendicular components

RESOLUTION RULE

along the line = V cos θ  ·  perpendicular = V sin θ

(θ measured between the vector and the line — the component next to the angle gets cosine.)

SOLVED · COMPONENTS

Aircraft at 50 m s⁻¹, 40° north of east: east = 50 cos 40° = 38.3 m s⁻¹; north = 50 sin 40° = 32.1 m s⁻¹

Subtracting vectors: A − B = A + (−B) — reverse the second, then add.

3 · Distance & Displacement

KEY DEFINITIONS

Distance (d): total length along the actual path — scalar, unit m.

Displacement (s): straight-line length from start to finish in a stated direction — vector, unit m.

Walkers take a winding 15 km route yet finish only 10 km from the start: distance = 15 km, displacement = 10 km at 030° (N30°E).

SOLVED · HALF CIRCLE

Particle moves A→B along a semicircle, radius 5.0 m: distance = πr = 16 m; displacement = diameter = 10 m, directed downwards.

COMMON TRAPOne full lap: distance = 2πR but displacement = zero → average velocity zero while average speed is not.

4 · Speed & Velocity

KEY EQUATIONS

v = d / t   ·   v = Δs / Δt   [unit m s⁻¹]

conversion: km h⁻¹ ÷ 3.6 = m s⁻¹

  • Average speed over a period; a speedometer reads instantaneous speed.
  • Constant velocity ⇒ straight line and constant speed.
  • Circular motion at constant speed ⇒ velocity changes continuously (direction).
  • Sign convention on a line: “+” one way, “−” the other.
SOLVED · OLYMPIC 10 000 m

Mo Farah: 10 000 m in 27 min 5.17 s = 1625.17 s → v = 6.15 m s⁻¹ (≈ 22.1 km h⁻¹)

5 · Acceleration

DEFINITION

a = Δv / Δt = (v − u) / t   [vector · m s⁻²]

Deceleration = acceleration directed opposite to velocity.

SOLVED

Sports car: rest to 70 km h⁻¹ (= 19.4 m s⁻¹) in 6.3 s → a = 19.4 ÷ 6.3 = 3.1 m s⁻²

CONCEPT CHECKVelocity can change at constant speed (circle — direction changes). Speed cannot change at constant velocity.

MEMORY TRICK“Gradient = how fast · Area = how far.” Negative gradient on s–t ⇒ moving backwards.

Curved graph? Draw a tangent.

Area under v–t = displacement

SIGN OF AREAArea above the t-axis = positive displacement; below = negative. Total distance = Σ|areas|; total displacement = signed sum (a ball thrown up and caught: displacement zero).

7 · The SUVAT Toolkit (uniform a only!)

Five letters: s displacement · u initial velocity · v final velocity · a acceleration · t time. Each equation omits exactly one:

EquationMissing
v = u + ats
s = ½ (u + v) ta
s = ut + ½ at²v
v² = u² + 2ast
s = vt − ½ at²u

Derivations (examiners love these)

D1 · definition of a

a = (v − u)/t ⇒ at = v − u ⇒ v = u + at

D2 · area of trapezium on v–t graph

s = average velocity × t = ½ (u + v) t  (uniform a ⇒ average v = (u+v)/2)

D3 · substitute D1 into D2

s = ½ (u + u + at) t ⇒ s = ut + ½ at²

D4 · eliminate t

t = (v − u)/a into D2: s = ½ (u+v)(v−u)/a ⇒ 2as = v² − u² ⇒ v² = u² + 2as

D5 · substitute u = v − at into D2

s = ½ (2v − at) t ⇒ s = vt − ½ at²

Choosing the right equation — 4-step protocol

1. Write the suvat list: knowns, the target, and the quantity never mentioned.
↓
2. Is acceleration constant? If no → suvat forbidden (use graphs/energy).
↓
3. Pick the equation that omits the irrelevant quantity.
↓
4. Fix a sign convention (up = + ⇒ a = −9.81), substitute, solve, state direction.

8 · Free Fall & Measuring g

KEY FACT

In the absence of air resistance, all bodies fall with the same uniform acceleration g = 9.81 m s⁻² (≈ 10), downwards. (Galileo; suvat applies with a = ±g.)

Experiment: falling ball + light gates / stroboscope

  1. Release from rest (electromagnet removes reaction-time error).
  2. Record position vs time (light gates + timer, or stroboscope at e.g. 20 Hz ⇒ Δt = 0.05 s per flash, metre rule for scale).
  3. With u = 0: s = ½ g t² → plot s against t² ⇒ straight line through the origin.
  4. Gradient = ½ g ⇒ g = 2 × gradient.
DATA CHECKStrobe data: t = 0.30 s, s = 0.441 m ⇒ gradient = 0.441 ÷ 0.09 = 4.9 ⇒ g = 9.8 m s⁻² ✔

9 · Projectile Motion

THREE PRINCIPLES
  • Acceleration is always g, downwards → affects vertical motion only.
  • Horizontal velocity is constant (air resistance neglected).
  • Horizontal & vertical motions are independent — dropped and horizontally projected balls land simultaneously.
HORIZONTAL (θ = 0)

x = U t · y = ½ g t² · v_y = g t · vₓ = U

AT AN ANGLE θ

x = (U cos θ) t · y = (U sin θ) t − ½ g t²
v_y = U sin θ − g t · speed = √(vₓ² + v_y²)

RANGE ON LEVEL GROUND (derivation)

Land when y = 0: (U sin θ) t − ½ g t² = 0 ⇒ t = 2U sin θ / g. Then R = (U cos θ) t.

R = U² sin 2θ / g  ⇒  R(max) at θ = 45°, R = U² / g

AIR RESISTANCEDrag opposes motion and grows with speed → shorter range, lower maximum height, steeper descent than ascent.

10 · Worked Examples (examiner-marked style)

EXAMPLE 1 · VECTOR TRIANGLE

Ship sails 60 km north then 20 km east. AC = √(60² + 20²) = 63.2 km; θ = tan⁻¹(20/60) = 18.4° east of north.

EXAMPLE 2 · SUVAT (braking)

Car: u = 30 m s⁻¹, v = 0, s = 100 m. Use v² = u² + 2as: 0 = 900 + 200a ⇒ a = −4.5 m s⁻² (deceleration 4.5 m s⁻²).

EXAMPLE 3 · TWO-STAGE (link by impact speed)

Ball falls 0.85 m onto sand, impression 0.025 m deep.

Stage 1: v² = 2(9.81)(0.85) = 16.7 ⇒ v = 4.1 m s⁻¹ down. Stage 2: 0 = 16.7 + 2a(0.025) ⇒ a = −334 ⇒ deceleration ≈ 334 m s⁻².

EXAMPLE 4 · HORIZONTAL PROJECTION

Projected horizontally at 15 m s⁻¹ from a 35.0 m tower.

(a) t = √(2 × 35.0 / 9.81) = 2.67 s · (b) x = 15 × 2.67 = 40 m · (c) v_y = 26.2 ⇒ speed = √(15² + 26.2²) = 30.2 m s⁻¹.

EXAMPLE 5 · PROJECTION AT AN ANGLE

Arrow: 48 m s⁻¹ at 50°. u_y = 48 sin 50° = 36.8 m s⁻¹. Time to apex t = 36.8/9.81 = 3.7 s; H = u_y²/2g ≈ 69 m.

11 · Mistakes · Exam Tips · Memory Tricks

✗ Mistake✓ Correction
“At max height v = 0, so a = 0.”v = 0 but a = g = 9.81 m s⁻² — weight still acts.
Suvat across a bounce / changing a.Suvat only within one constant-a stage; link stages via impact speed.
Mixed signs for g.Declare convention first: up = + ⇒ a = −9.81 throughout.
Forgetting km h⁻¹ → m s⁻¹.Divide by 3.6.
Gradient of a curved s–t graph read directly.Draw a tangent; large gradient triangle with axis values.
Vector answer without direction.Always magnitude and direction (1 mark routinely lost).
Treating signed v–t area as distance.Signed area = displacement; distance = Σ|areas|.
MEMORY TRICKS
  • SUVAT: each equation hides one letter — choose the one hiding what you neither know nor need.
  • “Gradient = how fast · Area = how far.”
  • Projectile: “x runs at constant speed; y falls like a stone.”
  • g-graph: “s against t-squared: gradient is half of g.”
