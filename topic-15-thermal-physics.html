EXAMINER’S BRIEF

Everything in this topic hangs from the kinetic model: temperature measures average molecular kinetic energy, internal energy is the sum of all random kinetic + potential molecular energy, and heating can either raise temperature (kinetic up) or change state (potential up). Get the two numbers right — E = mcΔθ and E = mL — plus the Kelvin link T = θ + 273, and the graph-scaling method P = mc·gradient that flows straight into P1.

1 · States of Matter & Changes of State

Three states on the atomic scale, all described by the kinetic model of matter:

Solid — tightly bonded, vibrates in placeLiquid — still touching, free to flowGas — separated, rapid random motion
ChangeNameEnergy stored in…
solid → liquidmelting / fusionbreaking 1–2 bonds per molecule (potential)
liquid → gasvaporisation / boilingbreaking 8–9 bonds, huge separation (potential)
liquid → gas (below boil)evaporationfastest molecules leak off the surface → cools
gas → liquidcondensationreleases latent heat to surroundings
liquid → solidsolidificationreleases latent heat of fusion
EVAPORATION COOLS

Only the most energetic molecules escape a liquid’s surface. Those left behind have below-average kinetic energy — so the liquid cools. That is why wet skin in a breeze feels cold and alcohol swabs chill you.

BOILING vs EVAPORATION

Boiling happens throughout the liquid at a fixed temperature; evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature.

2 · Internal Energy & the Heating Curve

DEFINITION

Internal energy U of a system = the sum of the random distribution of kinetic and potential energies of its molecules.

kinetic = motion & vibration · potential = separation (bond energy, more negative when packed)

THE HARDEST IDEA

You can put energy into a substance without the temperature rising — during melting and boiling the energy goes into breaking bonds (potential energy), not speeding molecules. “Latent” means hidden.

AB · solid heated → molecules vibrate faster → KE ↑, PE ~ constant.
▼
BC · melts at 0 °C → disorder + breaking bonds → PE ↑, KE constant (temperature flat).
▼
CD · liquid heated → molecules dart faster → KE ↑.
▼
DE · boils → molecules tear free of every neighbour → PE ↑ greatly, temperature flat.
▼
EF · steam heated → particles fly faster → KE ↑.
MEASURE WITHOUT THE TEMP RISE?

It takes much more energy to boil water than to melt the same mass. Melting breaks 1–2 bonds per molecule; boiling frees it from ~8–9. So L_v ≈ 2.26 MJ kg⁻¹ while L_f ≈ 330 kJ kg⁻¹ — roughly a sevenfold difference.

3 · Temperature & Thermal Equilibrium

WHAT TEMPERATURE DOES

Thermal energy flows from higher temperature to lower temperature. When nothing flows, the objects are in thermal equilibrium (same temperature, no net transfer).

A thermometer shows its own temperature — wait until it stops changing before you trust it.

STOP THINKING “HOT” = “LOTS OF HEAT”

A swimming pool and a cup of water at the same temperature hold wildly different amounts of thermal energy — but because they have the same temperature, no energy flows between them.

Temperature does NOT equal heat. Temperature measures average molecular KE; internal energy measures the total random kinetic + potential.

4 · The Kelvin Scale & Absolute Zero

CONVERSIONS

T / K = θ / °C + 273.15   ·   θ / °C = T / K − 273.15

A 1 K rise = a 1 °C rise — it’s the offsets that differ, not the sizes.

ABSOLUTE ZERO

0 K = −273.15 °C, the lowest possible temperature. At absolute zero, it is impossible to remove any more energy; molecules have minimum internal energy (zero kinetic, minimum potential).

WHY KELVIN BEATS CELSIUS

The Celsius scale is defined by the water point (0/100 °C), which depends on pressure and impurities — and you must extrapolate with a tiny physical property. The Kelvin scale uses the founding idea that average KE is directly proportional to this scale, so it doesn’t depend on any particular substance.

FIXED POINTS

0 K: absolute zero. 273.16 K: the triple point of water (ice+water+vapour coexisting) = 0.01 °C. One kelvin is 1/273.16 of that triple point.

35 °C = ___ K

35 + 273.15 → 3 s.f. 308 K. A rise 27 °C → 150 °C means a rise of 123 K — degrees and kelvin intervals are the same size.

5 · Thermometers & Thermometric Properties

A thermometer works on a physical property that changes with temperature (the thermometric property of its thermometric substance).

ThermometerThermometric propertyNotes
liquid-in-glasslength / density of liquid columnnon-linear; only reliable at fixed points
resistance (thermistor)resistance R of a thermistorsensitive over a narrow range; fast
thermocouplee.m.f. between junctions of two metalswide range, small, quick — calibrate
constant-volume gaspressure p of gas at fixed volumemost linear; direct route to absolute zero
radiationcolour of heated wirevery high temperatures
EMPIRICAL SCALE

Set Pi at ice point, Ps at steam point, then an unknown θ satisfies:

θ = 100 × (Pθ − Pi) / (Ps − Pi) °C

Different substances agree only at the two fixed points unless linear!

GAS WINS

Gas thermometers agree with each other far better than liquid ones, and the agreement improves at lower pressures. Extrapolating p–θ to p = 0 lands at −273.15 °C = absolute zero. As the gas gets less dense, its line becomes more nearly straight … and T = 273.16 × p/ptr.

6 · Specific Heat Capacity

THE DEFINING

c = the energy required per unit mass to raise the temperature by 1 K (or 1 °C).

E = mcΔθ   [c] = J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹

specific = per unit mass. water 4200 · ice 2100 · copper 390 · lead 126

Materialc / J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹
water4200
ice2100
ethanol2500
aluminium~900
copper390
mercury140
WHY WATER IS SPECIAL

Water’s high c (~4200) means oceans and bodies store huge energy with only small temperature change — the reason coastal climates are mild and why coolant in engines is water.

CLASSIC SLY

Energy required to heat 5.0 kg of water 20 → 100 °C: E = 5.0 × 4200 × 80 = 1.7 MJ. Iron block: P = 50 W for 4 min heats 1.2 kg from 22 to 45 °C → c = Pt/mΔθ = 50×240/(1.2×23) ≈ 435 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.

7 · Measuring Specific Heat Capacity (P1 favourite)

The setup: an insulated metal block (mass m) heated by an electrical heater of known VI; record θ vs t.

THE GRAPH METHOD

E = mcΔθ ⇒ P = VI and divide by time:

P = mc × (Δθ/Δt) = mc × gradient

heat slowly so energy spreads; insulate; start a little below room temp

P = VI from ammeter + voltmeter (or a wattmeter)
▼
plot θ against t; the straight-line gradient is Δθ/Δt
▼
c = P / (m · gradient)
ERRORS & SIGNS

Heat escapes to surroundings ⇒ c measured too high (energy needed is over-counted). It’s mainly a systematic error: heat the block from below room temp so losses cross zero at room temp. The 50 W example of §6 fits exactly here — 4 A × 12 V ≈ 50 W.

8 · Specific Latent Heat

DEFINITIONS

L = energy per unit mass to change state at constant temperature (J kg⁻¹).

E = mL

fusion (melt) Lf :: vaporisation (boil) Lv

SubstanceLf / kJ kg⁻¹Lv / kJ kg⁻¹
water/ice3302260
ethanol108840
copper2054840
WHY Lᵥ ≫ Lꜰ

Vaporisation tears a molecule free of all neighbours, multiplying volume ~1800× (1 cm³ water → 1800 cm³ steam) and doing work against the atmosphere. Melting just breaks a few bonds.

E = mL vs E = mcΔθ

Temperature change → mcΔθ; state change at constant T → mL. Never combine them into one magic formula.

9 · Calorimetry — Heat Exchanges

THE GOLDEN EQUATION

heat lost by hot body = heat gained by cold body

(assuming no losses to surroundings — the ideal calorimeter)

WORKED · COFFEE & ICE

300 g coffee 25 °C, add 15 g ice at 0 °C (c = 4.2 kJ kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, L_f = 334 kJ kg⁻¹):
lost by coffee = gained by ice+water
0.30×4.2×(25−θ) = 0.015×334 + 0.015×4.2×(θ−0)
→ θ ≈ 18–19 °C.

WORKED · STEAM SOURDOUGH

Kettle 2.8 kW, 465 g water 24 °C, 92% efficient, 2.5 min: energy in = 0.92×2800×150 = 386 kJ; warm to 100 °C takes 0.465×4200×76 ≈ 148 kJ; rest = 238 kJ boils m = E/L_v = 238/2260 ≈ 0.105 kg.

10 · Worked Examples & Revision

SOLVED · SPECIFIC HEAT

26400 J into 2.0 kg aluminium, 20→35 °C → Δθ = 15 K. c = E/mΔθ = 26400/(2×15) = 880 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ ✓ textbook value.

SOLVED · STEAM

2.0 g → steam at 100 °C needs E = mL_v = 0.002 × 2.26×10⁶ = 4520 J.

FIRST LAW CROSS-CHECK (ch 17)

ΔU = q + W. Free-fire: heat 250 kJ in, 500 kJ of work compression ⇒ ΔU = +750 kJ. Compression W>0, expansion W<0. Insulated (adiabatic): q=0 ⇒ ΔU=W — how a bicycle pump heats.

FORMULA BANK

T/K = θ + 273 · ΔT(K) = Δθ(°C)
E = mcΔθ · E = mL
θ = 100(Pθ − Pᵢ)/(Pᵩ − Pᵢ)
first law ΔU = q + W

TRAP LIST

J vs J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹: c is per unit mass per degree · θ in °C vs T in K — temperature rising is the same size but never use °C in Perfect gas law · latent heat: no temp change · steam point vs triple point — don’t mix.
