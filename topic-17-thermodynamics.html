Thermodynamics

EXAMINER'S BRIEF

Thermodynamics is conservation of energy rewritten in molecular clothes. Master three quantities and how they shuffle: U (internal energy = total molecular K + P), q (thermal energy in), w (work done on the gas). Sign convention is the whole game — write it down before you start every question. Paper 4 loves pV-diagram cycles and the “state and explain” temperature question.

1 · Internal Energy of a System

DEFINITION

Internal energy U = the sum of the random distribution of kinetic and potential energies of all the molecules of a system.

Kinetic — from random motion · Potential — from positions & intermolecular forces

THE IDEAL GAS SHORT-CUT

An ideal gas has no intermolecular forces, so no potential energy:

U (ideal gas) = total kinetic energy only

U = N × ¾kT → rise proportional to T. This is why the P–Q cycle questions just use ΔU ∝ ΔT.

PICTURE IT

Each molecule’s K and P swing wildly about — but sum them over the whole gas and the randomness cancels: at constant temperature the total U is constant. Internal energy is fixed by the state of the gas, not its history.

2 · Temperature vs Internal Energy

TEMPERATURE

Average translational kinetic energy per molecule. Independent of how many molecules — a drop and a pool can share 20 °C.

INTERNAL ENERGY

Total kinetic energy (+ potential for real substances). Depends on the number of molecules — the pool holds far more U.

THE LINK

A rise in temperature → an increase in internal energy

For ideal gas, T ↑ ⇒ average KE ↑ ⇒ total U ↑. But watch out — during melting/boiling T stays flat while U still climbs (potential part), as in Topic 15.

3 · Work Done by a Gas — w = pΔV

Gas in a cylinder, frictionless piston of area A, constant pressure p (balanced by the outside atmosphere).

Force on piston F = p × A
▼
Piston moves distance x → work = F × x = pAx
▼
Ax = the increase in volume ΔV
CONSTANT PRESSURE

w = pΔV

p in Pa, ΔV in m³ → J

BY vs ON

If the gas expands it pushes the atmosphere — work is done BY the gas (energy leaves). If the gas is compressed the atmosphere does work ON the gas (energy enters). This distinction drives the sign convention of §4.

WORKED 16A

750 cm³ → 900 cm³ at 1.4×10⁵ Pa: ΔV = 150 cm³ = 150×10⁻⁶ m³; w = 1.4×10⁵ × 150×10⁻⁶ = 21 J (done by the gas).

4 · The First Law of Thermodynamics

SAY IT FIRST

Increase in internal energy ✓ thermal energy added to the system ✓ work done ON the system.

ΔU = q + w

ΔU rising ⇐ q in by heating ⇐ w in by work

CONSERVATION OF ENERGY

It’s your Topic-5 friend: energy can’t be created or destroyed, only transformed. Whatever enters by heating and by mechanical work must show up as internal energy. QED — no magic.

WORKED 16B

250 J thermal added, system does 100 J (so w = −100): ΔU = 250 − 100 = 150 J increase.

5 · Sign Conventions — the Trick

QuantityPositive means…Example
qthermal energy added to systemkettle element heating water
wwork done ON the systempiston compressed in
ΔUinternal energy increasesgas heats up
THE CLASSIC PITFALL

Some markschemes list work done by the gas positive instead. Always restate your convention before writing the equation, and translate “piston pushed in” to w = +, “gas expands” to w = −.

RAPID SELF-CHECK

Thermal added +, thermal leaves −. Work on +, work by −. U up +. Then any given figure plugs in mechanically.

6 · Adiabatic & Isothermal Changes

ADIABATIC (q = 0)

ΔU = w

No thermal energy enters/leaves — achieved by insulating, or by compressing fast.

Brisk bicycle pump heats up — that’s why. Diesel engines use this very trick to ignite fuel.

ISOTHERMAL (T = const)

ΔU = 0

(Ideal gas: U depends only on T.)

So 0 = q + w: every joule of heat in is emitted as work — or the other way. Temp never moves.

Adiabatic ↔ fast / insulated · Isothermal ↔ slow / cool-bath · Cycles: PQRP is a loop, so total ΔU = 0!

7 · The Kettle & the Diesel — applying the first law

Electric kettle: heating element delivers q > 0, no piston so w = 0 → ΔU = q. Water warms.
▼
Bicycle pump: compression is adiabatic (too fast for heat to escape) → ΔU = w > 0 → air heats.
▼
Diesel engine: air compressed so rapidly temperature spikes above fuel’s ignition point → fuel ignites.
WHAT MAKES IT ADIABATIC?

The pump becomes hot yet Q = 0 — because strokes are quicker than the heat can leak away. Insulation is nice, but speed does the job.

8 · Changes of State & Latent Heat

MELTING

Bonds break → potential energy rises, kinetic energy (T) unchanged. Most substances expand slightly → small external work. Heat supplied = latent heat of fusion.

VAPORISATION

Molecules separate hugely → potential enormously up; T flat. Volume change (e.g. 1 cm³ → 1600 cm³ steam) is far larger → external work pΔV dominates → L_v ≫ L_f.

WORKED 16C · EVAPORATION

L_v = 2300 J g⁻¹, 1.0 g evaporates, ΔV = 1600 cm³ = 1600×10⁻⁶ m³ against 1.0×10⁵ Pa:

work done = 1.0×10⁵ × 1600×10⁻⁶ = 160 J
ΔU = q + w = 2300 − 160 = 2140 J

GAUGING TEMPERATURE VIA MOLECULES

“State and explain the change in temperature”— answer always boils down to: T measures average molecular KE; changing direction of molecular speed distribution shifts T accordingly.

FORMULA BANK

TRAPS ⇔ U-TURN
