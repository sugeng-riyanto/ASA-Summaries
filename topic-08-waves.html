Waves transfer energy without transferring matter. Master the five wave parameters (amplitude, wavelength, period, frequency, phase difference), read graphs carefully, and remember that only transverse waves can be polarised. The wave equation v = fλ appears in nearly every waves question.

2 · Progressive Waves

DEFINITION

A progressive wave is a wave that travels outwards from its source, transferring energy from one place to another without transferring matter.

  • A vibrating object (source) sets the surrounding medium into oscillation.
  • Each particle passes energy to its neighbour; the particles themselves oscillate about a fixed position.
  • Mechanical waves (sound, water ripples, seismic) need a medium; electromagnetic waves do not.
KEY POINT

The red dots (particles) move up and down about their equilibrium positions, but the wave crest and the energy move to the right. No particle travels with the wave.

GRAPH NOTE

Both types of wave can be drawn as a displacement–distance graph with the same shape. The displacement axis shows particle displacement (up/down for transverse; back/forth for longitudinal).

PERIOD–FREQUENCY LINK

T = 1 / f

Phase difference

Phase difference compares the vibration states of two particles. One complete cycle = 360° = 2π rad.

PHASE DIFFERENCE

φ = (x / λ) × 360°   or   φ = (x / λ) × 2π rad

where x = separation of the two points along the wave.

  • Points in phase: φ = 0°, 360° (or 0, 2π rad) — same displacement, same direction of motion.
  • Points in antiphase: φ = 180° (π rad) — one at a crest, the other at a trough.

5 · Wave Equation v = fλ

DERIVATION

In one period T, the wave travels one wavelength λ.

Speed = distance ÷ time, so  v = λ / T.

Since f = 1/T:

v = f λ

SOLVED EXAMPLES

Sound: f = 170 Hz, λ = 2.0 m → v = 170 × 2.0 = 340 m s⁻¹.

Water wave: f = 5.0 Hz, λ = 0.080 m → v = 5.0 × 0.080 = 0.40 m s⁻¹.

Violin note: f = 500 Hz, v = 340 m s⁻¹ → λ = 340/500 = 0.68 m.

KEY POINT

When a wave passes from one medium to another, its frequency stays constant (set by the source) but its speed and wavelength both change.

6 · CRO Measurements

A cathode-ray oscilloscope displays a voltage–time trace. The two calibrated controls let you measure amplitude and frequency.

FREQUENCY

Measure the horizontal distance for one complete cycle (crest to crest).

T = (number of divisions) × (time-base setting).

Then f = 1 / T.

AMPLITUDE

Measure the vertical distance from the centre line to a crest (half of crest-to-trough).

Amplitude = (number of divisions) × (y-gain setting).

SOLVED EXAMPLE

Time-base = 2.0 ms cm⁻¹. One cycle spans 3.0 cm.

T = 3.0 × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 6.0 × 10⁻³ s → f = 1/(6.0 × 10⁻³) = 167 Hz ≈ 170 Hz.

Y-gain = 2.0 V cm⁻¹; amplitude spans 1.4 cm → amplitude = 1.4 × 2.0 = 2.8 V.

7 · Intensity

DEFINITION

Intensity is the power transferred per unit area, measured at right angles to the wave direction.

I = P / A   [W m⁻²]

INTENSITY–AMPLITUDE

I ∝ A²

For constant frequency, doubling the amplitude quadruples the intensity.

  • For a point source radiating equally in all directions, the energy spreads over a sphere of area 4πr², so I = P / 4πr². Intensity falls as 1/r².
SOLVED EXAMPLE

A wave has twice the intensity of another of the same frequency. Ratio of amplitudes:

A₁/A₂ = √(I₁/I₂) = √2 ≈ 1.4.

8 · The Doppler Effect (Sound)

When a source of sound moves relative to a stationary observer, the observed frequency differs from the source frequency.

DOPPLER EQUATION (SOUND)

f₀ = f · v / (v ± v_s)

f₀ = observed frequency · f = source frequency · v = speed of sound · v_s = speed of source

Use − when the source approaches (higher pitch); use + when it recedes (lower pitch).

SOLVED EXAMPLE

A police car approaches a stationary observer at 15 m s⁻¹, siren frequency 250 Hz, speed of sound 340 m s⁻¹.

f₀ = 250 × 340 / (340 − 15) = 250 × 340 / 325 = 262 Hz ≈ 260 Hz.

LIGHT TOO

Light also shows a Doppler shift: a source moving away is red-shifted (longer λ), a source approaching is blue-shifted (shorter λ).

9 · The Electromagnetic Spectrum

KEY FACTS
  • All EM waves are transverse waves.
  • All travel at the same speed c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ in free space.
  • Wavelengths 400–700 nm in free space are visible to the human eye.

Microwave f = 8.0 GHz → λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 8.0 × 10⁹ = 0.0375 m ≈ 3.8 cm.

10 · Polarisation & Malus's Law

DEFINITION

A transverse wave is plane-polarised when its vibrations occur in one plane only, containing the direction of travel. Only transverse waves can be polarised.

  • Unpolarised light vibrates in many planes at right angles to the direction of travel.
  • A polariser (e.g. Polaroid) transmits vibrations in one plane only, producing plane-polarised light.
  • A second polariser (the analyser) at 90° to the first blocks the light completely — they are "crossed".
MALUS'S LAW

I = I₀ cos²θ

θ = angle between the analyser's transmission axis and the plane of polarisation.

SOLVED EXAMPLE

Plane-polarised light of intensity I₀ passes through an analyser at 45°. Transmitted intensity:

I = I₀ cos²45° = I₀ × (0.707)² = 0.5 I₀ — the intensity is halved.

11 · Worked Examples & Exam Practice

EXAMPLE 1 · WAVE EQUATION

Sound of wavelength 1.7 m travels at 340 m s⁻¹. Frequency f = v/λ = 340/1.7 = 200 Hz.

EXAMPLE 2 · CRO

Time-base 0.50 ms cm⁻¹; three cycles occupy 7.2 cm. One cycle = 7.2/3 = 2.4 cm.

T = 2.4 × 0.50 × 10⁻³ = 1.2 × 10⁻³ s → f = 1/(1.2 × 10⁻³) = 833 Hz ≈ 830 Hz.

EXAMPLE 3 · DOPPLER

Ambulance siren 1500 Hz, moving at 30 m s⁻¹, sound speed 340 m s⁻¹.

Approaching: f₀ = 1500 × 340/(340 − 30) = 1645 Hz.

Receding: f₀ = 1500 × 340/(340 + 30) = 1383 Hz.

Difference = 1645 − 1383 = 262 Hz.

EXAMPLE 4 · INTENSITY

A point source emits power 100 W uniformly. Intensity at 2.0 m:

I = P/4πr² = 100/(4π × 2.0²) = 1.99 W m⁻² ≈ 2.0 W m⁻².

TRY YOURSELF (answers only)
  1. Radio wave λ = 250 m → f = 3.00 × 10⁸/250 = 1.2 MHz.
  2. X-ray f = 2.0 × 10¹⁸ Hz → λ = 3.00 × 10⁸/2.0 × 10¹⁸ = 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.15 nm.
  3. Two waves, same frequency, out of step by 0.25 s, period 2.0 s → phase difference = (0.25/2.0) × 360° = 45°.
  4. Amplitude ratio of two waves with intensity ratio 2:1 → √2 ≈ 1.4.
  5. Visible light 500 nm → f = 3.00 × 10⁸/5.0 × 10⁻⁷ = 6.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

12 · One-Page Revision

WAVE EQUATION

v = fλ
T = 1/f
frequency unchanged across media

WAVE PARAMETERS

Amplitude A = max displacement
Wavelength λ = distance between in-phase points
Phase φ = (x/λ) × 360°

TRANSVERSE vs LONGITUDINAL

Transverse: vibration ⊥ direction (light, EM)
Longitudinal: vibration ∥ direction (sound)
Only transverse can be polarised

INTENSITY

I = P/A [W m⁻²]
I ∝ A²
Point source: I = P/4πr²

DOPPLER (SOUND)

f₀ = f·v/(v ± v_s)
approach: − (higher pitch)
recede: + (lower pitch)

POLARISATION

I = I₀ cos²θ (Malus's law)
crossed polarisers (90°) → I = 0
only transverse waves polarise

KEY CONSTANTS & VALUES

c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ (all EM waves in free space) · visible λ = 400–700 nm · speed of sound ≈ 340 m s⁻¹ · 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m · 1 GHz = 10⁹ Hz

MEMORY TRICKS
  • EM spectrum (short → long λ): "Gamma X-rays Ultra-Violet Visible Infra-Red Micro Radio" — "Grizzly X-rays Upset Very Important Medical Researchers".
  • Doppler: "approach = minus = higher pitch".
  • Malus: "cos²θ" — amplitude uses cos θ, intensity uses cos²θ.
  • CRO: horizontal = time (period), vertical = voltage (amplitude).
