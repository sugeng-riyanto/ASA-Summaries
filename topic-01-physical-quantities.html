1 · Physical Quantities & the Scientific Method

DEFINITION

A physical quantity is a feature of something that can be measured — it consists of a numerical magnitude and a unit. “1500 in 200 s” is meaningless unless the distance unit is stated!

Observe & measure → record data

Scientific notation & significant figures

  • Standard form: 0.00034 = 3.4 × 10⁻⁴; 152 000 000 = 1.52 × 10⁸ (fewer zero-mistakes).
  • Sig figs: all non-zero digits count; trapped zeros count (20.052 → 5 s.f.); leading zeros never count (0.00034 → 2 s.f.).
  • Ambiguous trailing zeros are fixed by standard form: 6 × 10² (1 s.f.), 6.0 × 10² (2 s.f.), 6.00 × 10² (3 s.f.).

Estimation & orders of magnitude

The order of magnitude is the power of ten of a quantity. A sanity-check superpower: g ≈ 10 m s⁻², so a calculated “9800 m s⁻²” screams a power-of-ten slip.

Mass of a jar of peanut butter ≈ 5 × 10² g.  Whale-to-mouse mass ratio ≈ 10⁴ (4 orders); Sun-to-electron ≈ 10⁶¹ (61 orders)!

TWIN TRAPS

Areas & volumes square/cube the prefix: 1 mm² = 10⁻⁶ m²; 1 mm³ = 10⁻⁹ m³; 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³.

Spacing is meaning: “30 m s” = metre-seconds; “30 ms” = milliseconds; “30 m” = metres. No plural ‘s’ on units.

SOLVED · PREFIX ARITHMETIC

1.0 mg = 10⁻³ g and 1 µg = 10⁻⁶ g ⇒ 1.0 mg = (10⁻³ ÷ 10⁻⁶) = 1.0 × 10³ µg.  Also: 6.8 pF = 6.8 × 10⁻¹² F; 60 GW = 6 × 10¹⁰ W.

TABLE & GRAPH CONVENTION

Label columns and axes as quantity / unit (italic symbol, roman unit): t / s, v / m s⁻¹ — the data then appear as pure numbers. Avoid “t (s)” or “t in s”. A heading t / 10² s with entries 1, 2, 3 means 100 s, 200 s, 300 s.

HOMOGENEITY RULE

An equation is homogeneous (“balanced”) when every term has the same base units. Necessary, not sufficient: a balanced equation may still hide wrong pure numbers (½, 2π…).

SOLVED · PROVE HOMOGENEOUS

work = gain KE + gain GPE:  W = F·s → kg m s⁻² × m = kg m² s⁻²;  ½mv² → kg × (m s⁻¹)² = kg m² s⁻²;  mgh → kg × m s⁻² × m = kg m² s⁻². All terms match ⇒ homogeneous ✔

SOLVED · UNITS OF A CONSTANT

Drag: F = ½CρAv². Base units of ρAv² = kg m⁻³ × m² × (m s⁻¹)² = kg m s⁻² = units of F ⇒ C has no units.

Spring: W = ½kx² ⇒ k = W/x² → kg m² s⁻² ÷ m² = kg s⁻² (= N m⁻¹).

EXAMINER’S TEST“Show Pa = kg m⁻¹ s⁻²”: Pa = N m⁻² = (kg m s⁻²) m⁻² = kg m⁻¹ s⁻² ✔  Pressure = depth × density × g: m × kg m⁻³ × m s⁻² = kg m⁻¹ s⁻² ✔

DEFINITIONS

Accuracy: closeness of a measured value to the true value.  Precision: closeness of repeated readings to each other (small spread). A micrometer (±0.01 mm) with a zero error is precise but inaccurate.

DIGITAL METERSQuoted as e.g. ±1% ± 2 digits: a reading of 4.00 V ⇒ (4.00 × 0.01) + 0.02 = ±0.06 V, before any fluctuation uncertainty is added.

5 · Uncertainties & How to Combine Them

ESTIMATING ±

Uncertainty = the larger of: (i) the smallest division you can read, (ii) half the range of repeated readings. State ± to 1 s.f.; the value matches its decimal places: (22.9 ± 0.2) cm, never 22.92 ± 0.2. Stopwatch: reaction time ⇒ ±0.1 s minimum. A metre rule length needs two readings ⇒ ±1 mm total.

percentage uncertainty = (absolute ÷ measured) × 100%

COMBINING RULES — “ALWAYS ADD, NEVER SUBTRACT”
  • x = y ± z (add/subtract) → add absolute uncertainties.
  • x = y × z or y ÷ z → add percentage (fractional) uncertainties.
  • x = yⁿ → multiply the % uncertainty by n (so d² doubles, √ halves).
  • Mixed expressions → extreme-value method: Δx = (x_high − x_low)/2.
SOLVED · ADDITION

I = I₁ + I₂ = (2.0 ± 0.1) + (1.5 ± 0.2) ⇒ I = (3.5 ± 0.3) A.  Δθ = 21.5 − 20.0 = 1.5 °C, ±(0.5+0.2) ⇒ (1.5 ± 0.7) °C — small differences carry huge % uncertainty (design experiments to avoid them!).

SOLVED · POWERS (CYLINDER)

V = πd²l/4 with d = 2.5 ± 0.1 cm (4.0%), l = 7.6 ± 0.1 cm (1.3%). V = 37.3 cm³; %V = 2(4.0) + 1.3 = 9.3% ⇒ ΔV ≈ 3.5 ⇒ V = 37 ± 3 cm³.

SOLVED · PENDULUM g ± EXTREME VALUES

g = 4π²l/T²; l = 0.55 ± 0.02 m (3.6%), T = 1.50 ± 0.02 s (1.3%) ⇒ g = 9.7 m s⁻²; %g = 3.6 + 2(1.3) = 6.2% ⇒ g = (9.7 ± 0.6) m s⁻².

Check: g_low = 4π²(0.53)/(1.52)² = 9.1; g_high = 4π²(0.57)/(1.48)² = 10.3 ⇒ Δg = (10.3 − 9.1)/2 = 0.6 ✔

BETTER PRACTICEDon’t find g from one pendulum length: take many lengths, plot T² against l ⇒ gradient = 4π²/g. Graphs average out random error.

READING PRACTICE

Micrometer: barrel 9.5 mm + thimble 0.36 mm = 9.86 mm.  Zero error +0.12 mm, reading 15.62 mm ⇒ true length = 15.62 − 0.12 = 15.50 mm.  Vernier: main 5.5 cm + 3rd coincidence ⇒ 5.53 cm.

CHOOSING INSTRUMENTS · WORKED

Page of a book: 30 cm rule for height/width (±1 mm on ~210 mm ≈ 0.5%). Thickness of one page is hopeless even with a micrometer → measure 400 pages (≈18 mm ± 0.01 mm ≈ 0.05%) and divide — the % uncertainty carries to one page.

TECHNIQUE NOTES
  • Check zero before measuring; avoid worn rule ends — take a reading at each end and subtract.
  • View scales perpendicularly (parallax); mirror-behind-needle trick for analogue meters.
  • Time many oscillations (total ≥ 10 s) so reaction time becomes a small %; start timing at the centre of the swing (fiducial mark), not the ends.
  • Circuits: build the main series loop first, then add parallel meters.

7 · Scalars & Vectors

DEFINITIONS

Scalar: magnitude + unit only; adds algebraically. Vector: magnitude + unit + direction; adds geometrically (vector triangle/polygon).

Scalarsmass · speed · distance · time · density · energy · work · power · pressure · temperature
Vectorsdisplacement · velocity · acceleration · force · weight · momentum
EXAMINER’S FAVOURITE TRICKElectric current is a scalar — although we talk of its “direction”, it does not obey vector addition. Test any quantity: walk 3 min north + 3 min any other direction = always 6 min ⇒ time is a scalar; two equal opposite forces cancel ⇒ force is a vector.

Adding & subtracting coplanar vectors

  • Same line: same direction → add (6 N + 4 N = 10 N); opposite → subtract (2 N towards the 6 N).
  • At right angles: Pythagoras + tan θ.  General angle: resolve, or cosine rule; 3+ forces → closed polygon; equilibrium → closed triangle.
  • Subtraction: A − B = A + (−B) (reverse B, then add).
SOLVED · SHIP & CURRENT

R = √(12² + 4²) = √160 = 12.6 km h⁻¹; θ = tan⁻¹(4/12) = 18.4° east of north.  Likewise 10 N ⊥ 7 N ⇒ √149 = 12.2 N at tan⁻¹(7/10) = 35° to the 10 N force.

Resolution into perpendicular components

SOLVED · GLIDER CABLE

T = 620 N at 25° above horizontal: F_H = 620 cos 25° = 560 N; F_V = 620 sin 25° = 260 N.  Aircraft 310 km h⁻¹ at 35° E of N: north = 310 cos 35° = 254; east = 310 sin 35° = 178 km h⁻¹.

“Balanced ⇒ correct”Homogeneity is necessary, not sufficient (pure numbers unchecked).
MEMORY TRICKS
  • Prefix ladder: p n µ m | k M G T — “pico-nano-micro-milli, then kilo-Mega-Giga-Tera”, steps of 10³.
  • “Precise = repeatable · Accurate = truthful.”
  • N = kg m s⁻² because F = ma tells you so; every derived unit follows from its defining equation.
  • Component next to the angle gets cos (“cos-adjacent”).

PAST-PAPER STYLE Q&A

Q1. Which is an SI base unit: ampere, coulomb, newton, joule? → ampere.  Q2. Prefix p? → 10⁻¹².

Q3. v = k√(P/ρ): %v = 4, %P = 2, %ρ = 3 ⇒ %k = 4 + ½(2+3) = ±6.5% ≈ ±7% (nearest option ±9% if data differ — apply the rule, not the memory!).

Q4. Girl walks 1.5 m s⁻¹ for 60 s at 35° N of E; eastward distance = 90 sin 35°? Careful: east = (1.5 × 60) cos 35° = 90 × 0.819 ≈ 74 m (angle measured from east-axis as given “north of east” ⇒ cos for east).

Units first, units last — they carry the marks. ✔
