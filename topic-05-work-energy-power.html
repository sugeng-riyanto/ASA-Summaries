EXAMINER'S BRIEF

Work is the mechanism by which energy is transferred. Examiners reward precise use of the component of force in the direction of displacement, correct energy accounting, clean derivations of Eₖ and ΔEₚ, and careful use of efficiency and power.

1 · Overview & Concept Map

Work, energy and power are tightly linked: doing work transfers energy, and power measures how quickly that transfer occurs.

2 · Work Done by a Force

KEY DEFINITION

Work is done when a force moves its point of application in the direction of the force. Work done equals energy transferred.

W = F s

Unit: joule (J)  ·  1 J = 1 N m = 1 kg m² s⁻²

  • If the force and displacement are in the same direction, work done is positive.
  • If the force opposes displacement, work done is negative (e.g. braking force, friction).
  • If the force is perpendicular to displacement, no work is done by that force.
COMMON TRAP

Holding a heavy object stationary may feel tiring, but in physics no mechanical work is done on the object because there is no displacement in the direction of the supporting force.

SOLVED · SIMPLE WORK

A force of 300 N pushes a car 5.0 m in the direction of the force.

W = F s = 300 × 5.0 = 1500 J

3 · Work at an Angle & Force–Distance Graphs

GENERAL WORK EQUATION

W = F s cos θ

θ = angle between the force and the displacement.

SOLVED · PULLING AT AN ANGLE

A man pulls a box with a rope exerting 200 N at 30° to the horizontal. The box moves 5.0 m horizontally.

Horizontal component = 200 cos 30° = 173 N.

Work done = 173 × 5.0 = 866 J.

SOLVED · CHILD'S TOY

A child tows a toy with tension 1.5 N at 25° to the horizontal through 265 cm = 2.65 m.

W = 1.5 × 2.65 × cos 25° = 3.6 J

Force–distance graphs

GRAPH RULE

The area under a force–distance graph gives the work done. For a variable force, divide into strips or count squares.

4 · Energy & Conservation

ENERGY

Energy is the ability to do work. It is a scalar quantity and is measured in joules (J).

Form of energyAssociated with
Gravitational potential energyposition in a gravitational field
Kinetic energymotion
Elastic potential energystretched or compressed objects
Chemical energychemical reactions, fuels, food
Internal / thermal energyrandom molecular motion and temperature
Electrical energymoving charges in a potential difference
PRINCIPLE OF CONSERVATION OF ENERGY

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred from one form to another or from one object to another. In a closed system, total energy remains constant.

SANKEY DIAGRAMS

A Sankey diagram shows energy transfers. The width of each arrow is proportional to the energy transferred. The total width is conserved: useful energy + wasted energy = input energy.

5 · Gravitational Potential Energy

DERIVATION FROM W = Fs

To raise a mass m vertically at steady speed, the upward force must equal its weight: F = mg.

Work done = force × displacement = mg × Δh.

This work is stored as gravitational potential energy:

ΔEₚ = mgΔh

Valid near Earth's surface where g is approximately constant.

IMPORTANT

Δh is the vertical height change, not the distance travelled along a slope or curved path.

SOLVED · WEIGHT-LIFTER

A weight-lifter raises 200 kg through 1.5 m.

ΔEₚ = 200 × 9.81 × 1.5 = 2.94 × 10³ J

SOLVED · STACKING TINS

25 tins, each 472 g, are raised through 1.8 m.

Total mass = 25 × 0.472 = 11.8 kg.

ΔEₚ = 11.8 × 9.81 × 1.8 ≈ 210 J

6 · Kinetic Energy

DERIVATION FROM EQUATIONS OF MOTION

For constant mass m and constant resultant force F:

F = ma and W = Fs.

From v² = u² + 2as, rearrange: as = (v² − u²)/2.

Multiply by m: mas = ½m(v² − u²).

Since Fs = work done:

W = ½mv² − ½mu²

Work done by the resultant force equals the change in kinetic energy.

If the object starts from rest, u = 0, so:

Eₖ = ½mv²

EXAMINER TRAP

To find a change in kinetic energy, use v² − u². Do not square the change in speed, (v − u)².

SOLVED · CAR KINETIC ENERGY

A car of mass 900 kg travels at 20 m s⁻¹.

Eₖ = ½ × 900 × 20² = 1.8 × 10⁵ J

SOLVED · CHANGE IN KE

A car of mass 800 kg accelerates from 20 m s⁻¹ to 30 m s⁻¹.

ΔEₖ = ½ × 800 × (30² − 20²) = 400 × 500 = 2.0 × 10⁵ J

7 · Energy Transformations

NO LOSSES

If no energy is wasted:

loss in GPE = gain in KE

mgΔh = ½mv²

The mass cancels, so the final speed is independent of mass:

v = √(2gΔh)

SOLVED · PENDULUM BOB

A pendulum bob of mass 5.0 kg is released from a height 0.15 m above its lowest point.

Loss in GPE = 5.0 × 9.81 × 0.15 = 7.36 J.

At the lowest point: ½ × 5.0 × v² = 7.36 ⇒ v = 1.7 m s⁻¹

SOLVED · ROLLER COASTER WITH FRICTION

A 2500 kg train drops vertically 55 m along a 120 m track and reaches 30 m s⁻¹ at the bottom.

Loss in GPE = 2500 × 9.81 × 55 ≈ 1.35 × 10⁶ J

Gain in KE = ½ × 2500 × 30² = 1.13 × 10⁶ J

Work done against friction = 1.35 × 10⁶ − 1.13 × 10⁶ ≈ 2.2 × 10⁵ J

Average friction force ≈ (2.2 × 10⁵) / 120 ≈ 1.8 × 10³ N

8 · Efficiency

DEFINITION

efficiency = useful energy output / total energy input

It may also be written using power:

efficiency = useful power output / total power input

Efficiency is a ratio. It has no unit. As a percentage, multiply by 100%. Efficiency can never exceed 100%.

SOLVED · PULLEY SYSTEM

A man raises a load of weight 480 N through 3.5 m. He pulls with 200 N through 10.5 m.

Useful energy output = 480 × 3.5 = 1680 J.

Total energy input = 200 × 10.5 = 2100 J.

Efficiency = 1680 / 2100 = 0.80 = 80%

SOLVED · ELECTRIC MOTOR

A 60 W motor raises a 20 N load through 2.5 m in 8.0 s.

Useful energy output = 20 × 2.5 = 50 J.

Total energy input = 60 × 8.0 = 480 J.

Efficiency = 50 / 480 ≈ 0.104 = 10%

9 · Power

DEFINITION

Power is the rate of doing work, or the rate of energy transfer.

P = W / t = ΔE / t

Unit: watt (W)  ·  1 W = 1 J s⁻¹

DERIVATION OF P = Fv

Work done by a constant force: W = Fs.

Power: P = W/t = Fs/t.

For constant velocity, s/t = v, so:

P = Fv

This is especially useful for vehicles moving at steady speed.

SOLVED · SMALL MOTOR

An electric motor lifts a 1.5 N weight through 1.2 m in 2.7 s.

Work done = 1.5 × 1.2 = 1.8 J.

Power = 1.8 / 2.7 = 0.67 W

SOLVED · AIRCRAFT ENGINE

An aircraft engine exerts a force of 40 kN at a steady speed of 80 m s⁻¹.

P = Fv = 40 000 × 80 = 3.2 × 10⁶ W = 3.2 MW

SOLVED · TRAIN AT TOP SPEED

A train's motors provide 3.6 MW at 30 m s⁻¹.

Opposing force = P/v = 3.6 × 10⁶ / 30 = 1.2 × 10⁵ N

10 · Worked Examples & Exam Practice

EXAMPLE 1 · WORK AT AN ANGLE

A rope pulls a crate with force 250 N at 35° to the horizontal. The crate moves 8.0 m.

W = 250 × 8.0 × cos 35° ≈ 1.6 × 10³ J

EXAMPLE 2 · FALLING STONE

A stone falls from rest through 12 m. Neglect air resistance.

v = √(2gΔh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 12) ≈ 15 m s⁻¹

EXAMPLE 3 · BALL THROWN UPWARDS

A ball of mass 0.50 kg is thrown vertically upwards at 6.0 m s⁻¹.

Initial KE = ½ × 0.50 × 6.0² = 9.0 J

At maximum height, KE has become GPE: mgh = 9.0 ⇒ h = 9.0 / (0.50 × 9.81) ≈ 1.8 m

EXAMPLE 4 · PERSON CLIMBING

A person weighing 480 N climbs 10 m vertically in 12 s.

Work done against gravity = 480 × 10 = 4800 J.

Power = 4800 / 12 = 400 W

EXAMPLE 5 · CAR ENGINE POWER

A car engine provides 700 N at a top speed of 40 m s⁻¹.

P = Fv = 700 × 40 = 28 000 W = 28 kW

TRY YOURSELF (answers only)
  1. An electric fire rated 2.4 kW is on for 3.0 minutes. Energy = 2400 × 180 = 4.32 × 10⁵ J.
  2. A 60 kg boy runs up 22 steps, each 20 cm high, in 1.8 s. Power ≈ 1.4 kW.
  3. A 0.40 kg ball has speed 5.0 m s⁻¹. KE = 5.0 J.
  4. A 1200 kg car slows from 25 m s⁻¹ to 10 m s⁻¹. Loss of KE = 3.15 × 10⁵ J.
  5. A machine has useful power output 300 W and efficiency 75%. Input power = 400 W.

11 · Mistakes · Tips · Tricks

✗ Mistake✓ Correction
Using the full force when the force is angled.Use F cos θ, where θ is the angle between force and displacement.
Using path length instead of vertical height for GPE.Use the vertical height change Δh.
Calculating ΔEₖ using (v − u)².Use v² − u².
Forgetting that efficiency is a ratio.Efficiency has no unit; multiply by 100 only for a percentage.
Confusing work W with unit watt W.Use J for work/energy and W for power units.
Assuming P = Fv for accelerating systems without care.P = Fv is safest for constant velocity or instantaneous force and velocity in the same direction.
MEMORY TRICKS
  • Work: “force along displacement”.
  • Area under F–s graph: “area = work”.
  • Energy: “energy is never lost, only transferred”.
  • Efficiency: “useful over total”.
  • Power: “energy per second”; at steady speed, P = Fv.
