Atomic Structure & Particle Physics

EXAMINER’S BRIEF

One experiment gives you the whole theory: the α-scattering experiment proves the atom is mostly empty space with a tiny, massive, positive nucleus. Then everything is bookkeeping — conservation of nucleon number, proton number (charge) and mass–energy through every decay, and two families of particles (hadrons = made of quarks, leptons = fundamental).

1 · Inside the Atom

Ancient Greeks guessed “atomos” = indivisible. By 1869 Mendeleev’s periodic table needed a structure. J. J. Thomson proposed the plum pudding model: a positive “pudding” with negative electrons as “plums”.

PLUM PUDDING MODEL

Uniform ball of positive charge; electrons scattered throughout like plums. Neutral overall. Predicts:
α-particles pass straight through with little deflection.

RUTHERFORD’S MODEL

Almost all mass and all positive charge crushed into a tiny central nucleus (~10⁻¹⁵ m); electrons orbit far away; the atom is mostly empty space.

SCALE OF THINGS
  • proton & neutron radius ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m (1 fm)
  • nucleus radius ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ → 10⁻¹⁴ m
  • atom radius ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m
  • Electron mass ≈ 0.0005 × proton’s (essentially a speck)
GET A FEEL

Scale hydrogen up so the nucleus is a 1 cm marble — the orbiting electron would be a grain of sand ~ 800 m away. That’s how empty an atom is.

2 · The α-Particle Scattering Experiment

Rutherford, Geiger & Marsden (1911) fired a fine beam of α-particles at a very thin gold foil (~10⁻⁶ m) in a vacuum, and moved a scintillating screen around to count flashes at each angle.

α-particles aimed at gold foil in a vacuum
▼
Most (~99.99%) go straight through — atom is empty space
▼
Some are deflected at small angles — a concentrated positive charge exists
▼
About 1 in 20 000 deflected, more than 90° (bounce back) — positive charge is tiny & massive in the middle
WHY GOLD & A VACUUM?
  • Gold — can be hammered into a foil only a few hundred atoms thick.
  • Vacuum — α-particles are stopped by a few cm of air; it would scatter them first.
EXAMINER’S PUNCH LINE

The plum-pudding model cannot explain back-scattering — a diffuse, spread-out positive charge would bend α-particles gently like a bullet in water. A deflection > 90° demands a small, massive, positively charged nucleus at the atom’s centre (field so strong it acts like a “tin hat”, not a shallow “cymbal”).

3 · The Nuclear Model & Sizes

From the angles at which α-particles scatter, Rutherford made three deductions; the atom is mostly empty space, gains a small, dense, positively charged nucleus at its centre, and the positive charge is highly concentrated.

KEY SIZES

proton/neutron radius ≈ 0.8–1.0 fm (10⁻¹⁵ m)
nucleus ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ → 10⁻¹⁴ m  ·  atom ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m

  • Nucleus carries > 99.9% of the atom’s mass and all the positive charge.
  • Electrons contribute < 0.1% of the mass — the tiny mass of each electron comes nowhere near the energy budget.
  • Density of nuclear matter ≈ 10¹⁸ kg m⁻³ — the size of a grain of sand would carry the mass of a small mountain (~a million tonnes).
MUCHNESS

It helps to remember one little rhyme: “nuclee small, electron free, matter’s space is mostly empty.”

4 · Nucleons, Nuclides & Isotopes

DEFINITIONS

A (nucleon/mass number) = number of protons + neutrons.
Z (proton number) = number of protons.
N = number of neutrons. So A = N + Z.

Nuclide — a particular combination of protons & neutrons; written AZX.

Isotopes — same Z (same element), different N; chemically identical, differ in mass.

NuclideAZN = A − Z

WHY U-238/235?

Both are uranium (Z = 92), but U-238 has 146 neutrons, U-235 143 — a textbook pair of isotopes. Two types of nuclei have the same place in the periodic table.

TRADE-OFF

More ionising → less penetrating. α turns the most ionising damage, loses energy fastest → stops quickly. γ ionises least, penetrates everything. The inverse link is a classic explanation question.

REFERENCE

Identification: deflect α-particles strongly in E or B fields (helium ion He²⁺); β⁻ deflects opposite way (electron); γ undeflected (no charge, EM wave).

6 · Decay Equations & Conservation

In every nuclear reaction: nucleon number A and proton number Z (charge) are conserved; mass–energy conserved (the mass defect appears as KE + γ).

α-DECAY

Daughter: A − 4, Z − 2
e.g. ²³⁴₉₂U → ²³⁰₉₀Th + ⁴₂He

β⁻-DECAY

Same A, Z + 1
e.g. ²³⁴₉₀Th → ²³⁴₉₁Pa + ⁰₋₁ₑ + anti-ν

β⁺-DECAY

Same A, Z − 1
e.g. ³⁰₁₅P → ³⁰₁₄Si + ⁰₊₁ₑ + ν

γ

No change in A or Z — just the excess energy of the excited daughter nucleus released as a photon!

Parent nuclide → daughter nuclide + particle (+ energy)
▼
Add up the top numbers (A) each side — must match
▼
Add up the bottom numbers (Z) each side — must match
▼
Charge conserved + mass–energy conserved ⇔ equation balances
COMMON MISTAKE

In β⁻, Z increases by 1 (a neutron becomes a proton). In β⁺, Z decreases by 1 (a proton becomes a neutron). A never changes in β. A drops by 4 in α.

7 · The β Spectrum and Neutrinos

All the α-particles from one nuclide share the same kinetic energy; but β-particles have a continuous range of energies, from zero up to a maximum. Something must be carrying the missing energy...

WHY?

The full decay energy Emax is SHARED between the electron, the (new) daughter recoil and an undetected particle — the (electron) antineutrino in β⁻, or neutrino in β⁺. Their sum is constant: Eβ + Eν = Emax, but each alone varies → smeared spectrum.

FERMI'S LOSS

Named “little neutral one” by Enrico Fermi: zero charge, nearly zero mass, barely interacts — so it zooms through space almost unhindered. Its presence is inferred from the missing energy, not seen directly.

8 · Leptons — The Fundamental Family

WHAT ARE LEPTONS

Particles not affected by the strong nuclear force. Electron & neutrino and their antiparticles are leptons → fundamental (no constituents; so far unsplittable).

  • Leptons don’t feel the strong force that sticks nucleons together.
  • electron e⁻ must be in every atom; neutrino ν (zero charge, tiny mass) from β decay.
  • Each has an antiparticle with same mass, opposite charge (or charge 0).
  • Standard statement: “Leptons are fundamental; hadrons are composite.”
MEMORY

“Leo(p)ton” — light-weight. “Hadron” — the bulky, heavy ones. Learn force criterion: lepton ∉ strong force, hadron ∈ strong force.

9 · Quarks & Hadrons

Gell-Mann (1964) brought order to the hadron zoo: hadrons are made of even smaller particles called quarks — with charges of ⅓e and ⅔e (far weirder than any whole multiples), in six flavours.

SIX FLAVORS OF QUARK

up u (+2/3e)  ·  down d (−1/3e)  ·  charm c (+2/3e)
strange s (−1/3e)  ·  top t (+2/3e)  ·  bottom b (−1/3e)
each has an antiquark (opposite charge)

HadronCompositionCharge check
protonuud → ⅔ + ⅔ − ⅓ = +e✓
neutronudd → ⅔ − ⅓ − ⅓ = 0✓
π⁺ mesonu + anti-d → ⅔ + ⅓ = +e✓
π⁻ mesonanti-u + d → −⅔ − ⅓ = −e✓
TWO WAYS TO BUILD
  • baryon = 3 quarks (or 3 antiquarks) — proton, neutron, …
  • meson = quark + antiquark — π, K, φ, …
FRACTIONAL CHARGE!

Quarks are the only known particles with charge less than e. But they are never seen alone — confinement keeps them inside hadrons, so charge never travels as half a unit; only whole or zero net charge ever escapes.

10 · β Decay at the Quark Level

β⁻ — A DOWN TURNS UP

neutron (udd) → proton (uud) + e⁻ + anti-ν
one down quark changes into an up quark

β⁺ — THE REVERSE

proton (uud) → neutron (udd) as an up quark changes to down, emitting a positron and a neutrino

LEPTON NUMBER

Conserved! In β⁻, an electron (lepton number +1) is born together with an anti-ν (−1), so lepton number balances to zero each side — that’s why the anti-ν must exist.

11 · Antiparticles & Annihilation

ANTIPARTICLE

Every particle has an antiparticle: same mass, opposite charge. The positron e⁺ is the antiparticle of the electron e⁻. Antimatter is built from antiparticles.

  • When matter meets antimatter (say e⁻ + e⁺) → annihilation: both vanish → two γ-photons. The two masses become pure energy (E = mc²).
  • Conservation: total charge stays 0, momentum is conserved (the two γ-photons fly out back-to-back), lepton number conserved (e⁻ = +1, e⁺ = −1 ⇒ sum 0).
WHY POSITRONS ARE “TRICKY”

A positron barely survives — it meets an electron almost immediately. In β⁺ decay, the pair quickly annihilates to two 511 keV γ-photons — the practical signal used in PET scans.

12 · Worked Examples & Revision

SOLVED · WRITING A DECAY

Th-232 → Ra (α) Ra has Z − 2 = 88, A − 4 = 228: ²³²₉₀Th → ²²⁸₈₈Ra + ⁴₂He ✓
Sr-90 β⁻: Z + 1 → ³₉Y; keep 90: ⁹⁰₃₈Sr → ⁹⁰₃₉Y + e⁻ + anti-ν ✓

MEMORY SNIPPETS

α: A−4, Z−2. β⁻: Z+1, A same. β⁺: Z−1, A same. γ: A and Z both unchanged — it just coasts out of the nucleus as a photon.

TRAP LIST

α slow and heavy; β has a spread of energies (spectrum); γ undeflected; strong force only binds hadrons; quarks never alone (confinement).
