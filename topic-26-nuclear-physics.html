EXAMINER'S BRIEF

Two halves: mass–energy (E = mc² → binding energy, fission, fusion) and the decay mathematics (A = λN, N = N₀e^(−λt), t½ = 0.693/λ). The binding-energy-per-nucleon graph is the key picture — everything about why stars shine and reactors work hangs off it.

1 · Balanced Nuclear Equations

Every decay/reaction obeys conservation of nucleon number A and proton number Z. The decaying nucleus is the parent; the product is the daughter.

DON'T CONFUSE

Nucleon number A ≠ neutron number N. In α decay A drops by 4 (two protons + two neutrons), so Z drops by 2 — check both sides of the equation, always.

2 · Mass & Energy: E = mc²

EINSTEIN'S EQUATION

E = mc²   ⇔   ΔE = Δmc²

c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹

Mass and energy are aspects of the same thing. Higher energy state ⇒ more mass; releasing energy makes mass fall. A kettle of hot water weighs more than cold — by a truly tiny amount — but in nuclear processes the changes become enormous.

SCALE

E = mc² with 1 g: 10⁻³ × (3×10⁸)² = 9 × 10¹³ J — the daily output of a large power station from a gram of matter. Mass-to-energy converts every second inside the Sun's core (it loses ~4 × 10⁹ kg s⁻¹).

3 · Mass Defect & the atomic mass unit

MASS DEFECT

Nuclear masses are measured in atomic mass units: 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg (defined as ¹⁄₁₂ the mass of a carbon-12 atom; ~934 MeV/c²).

The mass of a nucleus is less than the sum of its separate nucleons:

mass defect Δm = total mass of nucleons − mass of nucleus

WHY THE DEFECT?

Pulling nucleons apart means doing work against the strong nuclear force, pumping in energy and hence mass. So a bound nucleus (deeper in the "potential well") weighs slightly less than its free protons and neutrons. That lost mass is exactly the binding energy.

WORKED

Carbon-12: 6p + 6n should total 12.0989 u; the nucleus is 12.0000 u → mass defect ≈ 0.0989 u ≈ 0.0989 × 931 ≈ 92 MeV binding energy.

4 · Binding Energy per Nucleon

BINDING ENERGY

Binding energy = minimum energy needed to pull a nucleus completely apart into separate nucleons (equal to the mass defect × c²). No energy stored in the nucleus — energy you must put in.

binding energy per nucleon = binding energy / A

higher ⇒ more stable

READING THE GRAPH
  • Steep climb for light nuclei — helium sits well above its neighbours (very stable).
  • Broad peak near iron-56 (~8.8 MeV/nucleon) — the most stable nuclide in nature.
  • Gentle fall for heavy nuclei — the big, neutron-rich ones are looser and unstable.

5 · Fission & Fusion

FISSION

A heavy nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei of roughly equal mass. U-235 captures a slow neutron → unstable U-236 → barium + krypton + 2–3 neutrons + energy:

²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴¹₅₆Ba + ⁹²₃₆Kr + 3 ¹₀n

The emitted neutrons can trigger more fissions — a chain reaction, controlled by absorbing rods in a reactor.

FUSION

Two light nuclei join to make a heavier one — the Sun's energy source:

²₁H + ¹₁H → ³₂He + energy

Needs extreme temperature/pressure (thermonuclear conditions in stars) to overcome Coulomb repulsion between positive nuclei.

WHY ENERGY IS RELEASED

Both processes push nucleons up the binding-energy-per-nucleon curve towards iron. The products are more tightly bound, so their total binding energy exceeds the input's — and the difference is liberated. Fission climbs from the right, fusion from the left.

KEY FACTS TO QUOTE

Fission: heavy → two mid-mass fragments; releases ~200 MeV per U-235 nucleus; the energy appears as kinetic energy of the fragments (then heat). Fusion: lightweight joining; how the Sun shines; joining light → heavier product.

6 · Spontaneous & Random Decay

RANDOM

You cannot predict which nucleus will decay, or when. Each undecayed nucleus has the same constant probability of decaying per unit time, independent of its neighbours — like dice: you predict the number of sixes, never the exact dice.

SPONTANEOUS

Decay is unaffected by temperature, pressure, chemical state, or any external factor. A nucleus just "makes up its own mind", no matter how much you heat or squeeze it.

COUNT RATE FLUCTUATIONS

A GM tube clicks irregularly at a steady source; a ratemeter needle flickers. These fluctuations are the evidence of randomness. Take long averages to smooth them out — the underlying curve is a smooth exponential drop.

7 · Activity & the Decay Constant

THE KEY QUANTITIES

Activity A = number of nuclei that decay per unit time; unit becquerel (Bq) = 1 s⁻¹.

Decay constant λ = probability per unit time that a single nucleus decays (units s⁻¹).

A = λN

N = number of undecayed nuclei at that instant

MEASURED vs TRUE

A GM-tube count rate R is a fraction of the true activity — the detector misses radiation and background must be subtracted. R ∝ A, so R follows the same exponential law.

8 · Exponential Decay & Half-Life

DECAY LAW

x = x₀e^(−λt)

x = activity A, count rate R, or undecayed nuclei N

An isotope's curve never quite reaches zero — measured whole-sample half-lives do the talking. The rate of decay falls off because fewer nuclei remain to decay.

HALF-LIFE

The half-life t½ is the mean time for half the undecayed nuclei to decay (so A and R also halve each t½).

t½ = 0.693/λ   ⇔   λ = 0.693/t½

after n half-lives the fraction left = (½)ⁿ

UNITS DISCIPLINE

λ must match t: λ in s⁻¹ ↔ t in s; λ in year⁻¹ ↔ t in years. Convert before you substitute — a mismatched unit kills the answer.

9 · Worked Examples

ENERGY FROM FISSION

U-238 (237.9997 u) → fragments with mass defect 1.936 u. Per nucleon: ΔE/nucleon = (1.936 × 931)/238 ≈ 7.6 MeV per nucleon.

ACTIVITY

Phosphorus-32, A = 5.0×10⁶ Bq, λ = 5.6×10⁻⁷ s⁻¹ → N = A/λ = 5.0×10⁶/5.6×10⁻⁷ = 8.9×10¹² nuclei.

HALF-LIFE

Francium-221, t½ = 4.8 min. After 14.4 min = 3 half-lives → remaining fraction (½)³ = 1/8.

DECAY LAW

λ = 0.02 s⁻¹, N₀ = 1.0×10¹⁵, t = 20 s → N = 1.0×10¹⁵ e^(−0.4) ≈ 6.7×10¹⁴.

TRAP LIST
  • ✗ forgetting binding energy is energy put in, not stored
  • ✗ confusing A (nucleon number) with N (neutron count)
  • ✗ using units mismatched: λ in s⁻¹ vs t in years
  • ✗ forgetting to subtract background radiation for count rates
  • ✗ defining half-life as "time to decay completely" — it halves, never reaches zero
